When

Makar Sankranti 2027

Makar Sankranti is a solar festival: the day the sun enters Makara (Capricorn). That is why it stays close to 14–15 January every year. In 2027 it is Friday 15 January in India and Thursday 14 January on the US East Coast.

🪁 Make a free greeting card

  1. Thu 14 Jan
    Bhogi (Tamil Nadu, Andhra) · Lohri eve

    Old things go into a bonfire to welcome the new. Punjab celebrates Lohri with a bonfire, songs and revdi.

  2. Fri 15 Jan
    Makar Sankranti · Thai Pongal · Uttarayan

    The main day: a dip or bath at sunrise, prayers to Surya, til-gud and khichdi, kites in Gujarat, and Pongal rice boiled over in Tamil homes.

  3. Sat 16 Jan
    Mattu Pongal · Kanuma

    Cattle are bathed, decorated and thanked for their work in the fields.

Dates follow Drik Panchang for New Delhi and Raleigh, North Carolina. Your local temple may differ by a day, because tithis are calculated for local time.

Why we celebrate

The stories behind Makar Sankranti

Bhishma waits for the sun

Wounded on a bed of arrows in the Mahabharata war, Bhishma, who could choose the time of his death, waited for uttarayana, the sun's northern journey, which tradition calls the auspicious half of the year.

Read more →

Thank you, Surya

Every harvest depends on the sun. Sankranti is a thank-you: to the sun, the earth, the rain, the cattle and the farmers who grow our food.

Ganga reaches the sea

Bhagiratha brought the Ganga down to earth, and tradition says that on this day she reached the sea at Ganga Sagar in Bengal, where lakhs of pilgrims bathe every Sankranti.

One festival, many names

Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Uttarayan in Gujarat, Lohri in Punjab, Magh Bihu in Assam, Poush Sankranti in Bengal, Khichdi in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, Sankranti in Andhra, Telangana, Karnataka and Maharashtra.

At home

Celebrate Makar Sankranti at home

Every family and region has its own way, so follow your elders where you can. This is a simple outline anyone can follow.

  1. Greet the sunrise

    Bathe early, then offer water to the rising sun from a small pot, saying the 12 names of Surya below.

  2. Til and gud

    Make or buy sesame-and-jaggery sweets. In Maharashtra people say "tilgul ghya, goad goad bola": take this sweet and speak sweetly.

  3. Cook the new harvest

    Make khichdi or sweet Pongal with new rice, milk and jaggery, and let it boil over: a sign of plenty.

  4. Give

    Sankranti is a traditional day for giving: til, blankets, grain or a donation to a food bank.

The 12 names of Surya

Surya Namaskar mantras (traditional)

ॐ मित्राय नमः । ॐ रवये नमः । ॐ सूर्याय नमः ।
ॐ भानवे नमः । ॐ खगाय नमः । ॐ पूष्णे नमः ।
ॐ हिरण्यगर्भाय नमः । ॐ मरीचये नमः । ॐ आदित्याय नमः ।
ॐ सवित्रे नमः । ॐ अर्काय नमः । ॐ भास्कराय नमः ॥

Translation

Salutations to the friend of all, the shining one, the one who sets all in motion, the radiant one, the traveller of the sky, the nourisher, the golden source of creation, the source of rays, the son of Aditi, the giver of life, the one worthy of praise, and the maker of light.

In simple words

These are the 12 names said with the 12 rounds of Surya Namaskar. On Sankranti, say one name with each offering of water, or do one round of sun salutations for each name.

Kids' corner

Makar Sankranti with children

Kite day

Fly a kite with an adult, in an open field away from wires. Never use glass-coated string; it hurts birds and people.

Draw a kolam

Draw a kolam or rangoli with rice flour at the door, with a pot of Pongal in the middle.

Sun experiment

Mark where the sun rises on your horizon this week and again in March. Watch it move north!

Thank a farmer

Draw a thank-you card for the people who grow our food.
