Temples

25 great Hindu temples

Twenty-five temples from the Himalayas to the southern tip of India, and beyond to Nepal, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia. For each: amazing facts, the story behind it and tips for a visit. Tap "See photos" for real pictures; the drawings show each temple's architectural style.

A temple (mandir) is seen as the home of the deity on earth. Its tower points to the sky like a mountain, and its dark inner shrine, the garbhagriha or "womb house," is where the deity lives. Where a popular belief has not been proven, we say so.

5 temples

North India & the Himalayas

1

Kashi Vishwanath

Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh · Shiva as Vishwanath, Lord of the Universe

Amazing facts

  • It is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas, and Varanasi is often called one of the oldest continuously lived-in cities in the world.
  • The present temple was built in 1780 by Queen Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore, after the earlier temple was destroyed.
  • Its spires are covered in gold donated by Maharaja Ranjit Singh of the Sikh Empire in 1835, about one tonne of it, which is why it is called the Golden Temple of Varanasi.
  • The Kashi Vishwanath Corridor, opened in December 2021, links the temple directly to the Ganga ghats.
The story

Tradition says Kashi rests on the tip of Shiva's trident and is never destroyed, even at the end of an age. It is believed that Shiva whispers the liberating mantra into the ear of those who die here, so many come to Kashi to spend their last days.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Big days: Mahashivratri, Shravan Mondays (July–August), Dev Deepawali. Tips: queues can be long; phones and bags are usually not allowed inside, so use the lockers. Combine with the evening Ganga aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

2

Kedarnath

Rudraprayag, Uttarakhand · Shiva

Amazing facts

  • It stands about 3,583 m (11,755 ft) high in the Himalayas, the highest of the 12 Jyotirlingas.
  • It is open only about six months a year (around late April or May to November). In winter the deity is carried down to Ukhimath and worshipped there.
  • In the 2013 floods a huge boulder stopped just behind the temple and split the water around it. The temple survived; the rock is now worshipped as Bhim Shila.
  • The great teacher Adi Shankaracharya is believed to have left his body here; his samadhi shrine stands behind the temple.
The story

After the Mahabharata war, the Pandavas wanted Shiva's forgiveness. Shiva avoided them and hid as a bull. When Bhima recognised him, the bull sank into the ground, leaving its hump at Kedarnath. Other parts appeared at four more places, together called the Panch Kedar.

Planning a visit

Season: roughly late April/May to early November; opening and closing dates are announced each year. The last stretch is a steep trek of about 16–18 km from Gaurikund (ponies, palkis and helicopters are available). Register for the Char Dham yatra on the Uttarakhand government's site (registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in) before going, carry warm clothes and allow a day to adjust to the altitude.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

3

Badrinath

Chamoli, Uttarakhand · Vishnu as Badrinarayan

Amazing facts

  • It is one of the four Char Dham of India, set at about 3,100 m on the Alaknanda river.
  • Right below the icy mountains is Tapt Kund, a natural hot spring where pilgrims bathe before darshan.
  • The chief priest, the Rawal, is by tradition a Namboodiri Brahmin from Kerala, at the other end of India, a custom linked to Adi Shankaracharya.
  • The deity is a black stone image of Vishnu seated in meditation, not standing or reclining as in most Vishnu temples.
The story

Vishnu came here to meditate in the cold. To shelter him from the snow, Lakshmi became a badri (Indian jujube) tree and stood over him. Pleased, Vishnu named the place Badrinath, Lord of the Badri tree.

Planning a visit

Season: roughly late April/May to November; dates are announced each year. Reachable by road. Mana village, about 3 km away, is signposted as "India's first village" near the Tibet border. In winter the deity is worshipped at Jyotirmath. Register for the Char Dham yatra on the Uttarakhand government's site and pack warm layers.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

4

Vaishno Devi

Katra, Jammu & Kashmir · The Goddess as Mata Vaishno Devi

Amazing facts

  • The shrine is a cave in the Trikuta mountains at about 1,580 m (5,200 ft). There is no carved idol: the Goddess is worshipped as three natural rock forms, the pindis.
  • The three pindis are Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati: power, prosperity and wisdom.
  • Pilgrims walk about 12 km uphill from Katra, day and night. It is one of the most visited shrines in India: the Shrine Board counted about 9.5 million pilgrims in 2023.
The story

Vaishnavi, a devoted girl, went to the mountains to meditate. The proud Bhairon Nath chased her. She hid in a cave for nine months (Garbh Joon), then took the form of the Goddess and defeated him. Before dying he asked for forgiveness, and she blessed him: pilgrims also visit the Bhairon temple to complete the yatra.

Planning a visit

Open all year; Navratri (spring and autumn) is the busiest. Every pilgrim needs the free RFID Yatra card, issued at the Shrine Board's counters in Katra (you can register online first), and must cross the Banganga checkpoint within six hours of getting it. Battery cars, ponies, a ropeway near Bhawan and helicopters to Sanjhichhat help those who cannot walk the full way.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

5

Swaminarayan Akshardham

New Delhi · Bhagwan Swaminarayan

Amazing facts

  • Opened in 2005, it was recognised by Guinness World Records in 2007 as the world's largest comprehensive Hindu temple at the time.
  • The main monument is built of pink sandstone and white marble with no steel in its structure, following traditional temple-building texts.
  • It has 234 carved pillars, nine domes and a plinth of 148 life-size stone elephants, plus a short boat ride through India's history.
The story

Akshardham means "the abode of the eternal." It was built by the BAPS Swaminarayan Sanstha, with thousands of volunteers and craftsmen, to honour Bhagwan Swaminarayan (1781–1830) and to present India's art, values and spiritual heritage in one place.

Planning a visit

Usually closed on Mondays; check timings before going. Phones, cameras and bags are not allowed inside (free cloakroom). Dress modestly, covering shoulders and knees. Stay for the evening water show.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

4 temples

West & central India

6

Somnath

Prabhas Patan, Gujarat · Shiva

Amazing facts

  • Somnath is traditionally counted as the first of the 12 Jyotirlingas.
  • It was attacked and destroyed many times, famously by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1026, and rebuilt again and again. The present temple was rebuilt after Independence and inaugurated by President Rajendra Prasad on 11 May 1951, when the jyotirlinga was installed.
  • Near the sea wall stands the Baan Stambh (arrow pillar). Its Sanskrit inscription says the path over the sea from here to the South Pole is unobstructed. A line due south does cross open ocean almost all the way, passing only the small Kerguelen Islands near 49°S. (The inscription's age is uncertain.)
The story

The Moon god Soma married the 27 daughters of Daksha but loved only Rohini. Angry, Daksha cursed him to waste away. Soma prayed to Shiva here, and Shiva eased the curse so the Moon waxes and wanes instead of disappearing. That is why the place is called Somnath, Lord of the Moon.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Big days: Mahashivratri and Kartik Purnima (a large fair). The evening sound-and-light show by the sea tells the temple's history. Phones are usually deposited before entry.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

7

Dwarkadhish

Dwarka, Gujarat · Krishna as King of Dwarka

Amazing facts

  • It is one of the four Char Dham, in the city tradition says Krishna built after leaving Mathura.
  • The main shrine, Jagat Mandir, is five storeys tall, rests on 72 pillars and its spire rises about 78 m.
  • A huge flag on top, made from 52 yards of cloth, is changed several times every day, and families book years ahead for the honour of offering it.
  • Divers have found old stone structures and anchors underwater off the Dwarka coast. Many link them to Krishna's city, though archaeologists date most of them to much later, historical times.
The story

To protect his people from repeated attacks on Mathura, Krishna moved the Yadavas to the western sea and built a golden city, Dwarka. After Krishna left the world, the Mahabharata says the sea swallowed the city.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Janmashtami is the biggest festival. Nearby: Bet Dwarka island (now linked by the Sudarshan Setu bridge), Nageshwar Jyotirlinga and the Gomti ghat where the river meets the sea.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

8

Kailasa Temple, Ellora

Ellora, Maharashtra · Shiva (Mount Kailash in stone)

Amazing facts

  • The whole temple, with its towers, halls, bridges and life-size elephants, was carved out of one single rock, from the top down.
  • A commonly quoted estimate is that about 200,000 tonnes of rock were removed to free the temple from the hill.
  • It was made in the 8th century and is usually credited to the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I (reigned about 756–773). It is part of the Ellora Caves UNESCO World Heritage Site, where Hindu, Buddhist and Jain caves stand side by side.
The story

The temple is a picture of Mount Kailash, Shiva's home. One famous panel shows Ravana trying to lift the mountain, while Shiva calmly presses it down with his toe and Parvati holds on to him.

Planning a visit

The Ellora caves are closed on Tuesdays; best time is June to March (the monsoon makes the area green). Go early; climb the path above the temple to see it from the top. About 30 km from Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad).

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

9

Khajuraho

Chhatarpur, Madhya Pradesh · Shiva, Vishnu and others; also Jain temples

Amazing facts

  • Built by the Chandela kings, mostly in the 10th and 11th centuries. Records mention 85 temples by the 12th century; about 20–25 survive.
  • The largest, Kandariya Mahadeva, has towers rising one above another like the peaks of a mountain range.
  • The walls show every part of life: gods, musicians, dancers, warriors, animals and daily work. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
The story

A local legend says the Chandela dynasty began when the Moon god fell in love with Hemavati, and their son Chandravarman founded the dynasty that built these temples. The temples were partly hidden by forest for centuries, which helped protect them.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. The Khajuraho Dance Festival is held every February. Watch the evening light-and-sound show. Matangeshwar temple in the complex is still in active worship.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

East India

10

Jagannath, Puri

Puri, Odisha · Jagannath (Krishna) with Balabhadra and Subhadra

Amazing facts

  • The deities are made of wood and are replaced in a secret ceremony called Nabakalebara, held in years with an extra lunar month, usually 12 or 19 years apart (last in 2015).
  • Every year the deities ride out on huge wooden chariots in the Rath Yatra. The English word "juggernaut" comes from Jagannath.
  • Its kitchen is one of the largest in the world. Food is cooked in clay pots stacked on top of each other over wood fires and becomes Mahaprasad.
  • A priest climbs the tall tower every evening to change the flag. Popular belief also says the flag flies against the wind; this has not been scientifically shown.
The story

King Indradyumna wished to see Vishnu. A divine log floated ashore, and an old carpenter (Vishwakarma in disguise) agreed to carve it, on condition that no one opened the door for 21 days. The impatient queen opened it early; the carpenter had vanished, leaving the deities with round eyes and unfinished hands, the form worshipped to this day.

Planning a visit

Rath Yatra is in June or July. By temple tradition, only Hindus (and Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists of Indian origin) may enter; others can view the temple from the rooftop of the Raghunandan Library opposite. Phones, leather items and cameras are not allowed inside.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

11

Konark Sun Temple

Konark, Odisha · Surya, the Sun god

Amazing facts

  • The temple is shaped like the Sun god's giant chariot, with 24 carved stone wheels pulled by 7 horses.
  • The wheels work as sundials: the shadow of the spokes tells the time of day.
  • Built around 1250 CE by King Narasimhadeva I. Sailors once called it the Black Pagoda and used it as a landmark. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and appears on the Indian ₹10 note.
The story

Krishna's son Samba was struck by a skin disease. He prayed to the Sun god for twelve years near the sea at Konark and was healed, so a temple to Surya was raised here.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. The Konark Dance Festival is held every year from 1 to 5 December, in an open-air theatre facing the temple. There is no daily worship inside today; it is a protected monument, though pilgrims gather nearby for the Chandrabhaga Mela. About 35 km from Puri, an easy day trip.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

12

Kamakhya

Guwahati, Assam · The Goddess as Kamakhya (Shakti)

Amazing facts

  • It is one of the most important Shakti Peethas and a major centre of Tantric worship.
  • There is no idol. The Goddess is worshipped as a natural rock in a dark cave chamber, kept moist by an underground spring.
  • Its beehive-shaped dome is a style of its own, called the Nilachal type, after the hill it stands on.
  • Every June the temple closes for three days during the Ambubachi Mela, celebrating the Earth's and the Goddess's power of creation.
The story

When Sati gave up her life at her father Daksha's sacrifice, a grief-stricken Shiva carried her body across the world. To end his sorrow, Vishnu's discus divided the body, and the places where parts fell became the Shakti Peethas. Kamakhya is where the womb fell, so it is honoured as the seat of creation.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to April; Ambubachi Mela (June) and Durga Puja are very crowded. Queues are long; a paid express queue is available. On Nilachal hill, about 8 km from central Guwahati.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

South India

13

Tirumala Venkateswara

Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh · Vishnu as Venkateswara (Balaji)

Amazing facts

  • It is one of the most visited and richest temples in the world, with more than 60,000 pilgrims on an average day.
  • Many pilgrims offer their hair as a sign of giving up ego. The temple is one of the largest hair donors in the world.
  • Since 2009 the Tirupati laddu prasadam has a Geographical Indication (GI) tag, so no one else may sell a laddu under that name.
  • The temple sits on the seven hills of Tirumala, said to be the seven hoods of the serpent Adishesha.
The story

Vishnu came to earth as Srinivasa and wished to marry Princess Padmavati. For the grand wedding he took a huge loan from Kubera, the god of wealth. Devotees' offerings are said to help him repay it, which is why people give generously at Tirupati.

Planning a visit

Open all year; book darshan tickets online in advance on the official TTD site. Dress modestly for darshan (no shorts or sleeveless tops; traditional Indian clothes are preferred). For sevas, traditional dress is required: a dhoti with upper cloth for men, a saree or salwar with dupatta for women. You can walk up the hill by the Alipiri footpath (about 3,500 steps) or go by road. Brahmotsavam (usually September–October) and Vaikuntha Ekadasi are the biggest festivals.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

14

Meenakshi Amman

Madurai, Tamil Nadu · Meenakshi (Parvati) and Sundareswarar (Shiva)

Amazing facts

  • The temple has 14 gopurams (gateway towers); the tallest, the southern tower, is about 52 m high.
  • The towers are covered in thousands of brightly painted figures of gods, demons, animals and heroes.
  • The Hall of a Thousand Pillars actually has 985 carved pillars, and some pillars near it ring with musical notes when tapped.
  • Here the Goddess, not the God, comes first: pilgrims traditionally visit Meenakshi before Sundareswarar.
The story

Meenakshi was born to the Pandya king as an answer to his prayers, from a sacred fire. She grew up to be a fearless warrior queen and conquered the world, until she met Shiva on Mount Kailash. Shiva came to Madurai as Sundareswarar to marry her, and Vishnu gave her away as her brother.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. The Chithirai festival (April–May) re-enacts the divine wedding and draws huge crowds. Dress modestly; phones are not allowed inside. Visit at night for the palliyarai ceremony, when Shiva is carried to Meenakshi's chamber.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

15

Brihadeeswarar

Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu · Shiva

Amazing facts

  • Built by the Chola emperor Raja Raja Chola I and completed in 1010 CE; it is still in worship after more than 1,000 years.
  • Its tower (vimana) rises about 60 m (sources give 60–66 m), and near the top sits a single granite block estimated at about 80 tonnes.
  • It is built of granite, which is not found around Thanjavur; the stone was brought from quarries about 50 km away, possibly by river.
  • A popular claim says the tower casts no shadow at noon. This is a myth: like any tall building, it does cast a shadow.
The story

Raja Raja Chola built the temple as a statement of devotion and of the might of the Chola empire. The walls carry inscriptions listing gifts, temple staff, dancers and musicians, giving a rare picture of life 1,000 years ago. It is one of the Great Living Chola Temples, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Go in the late afternoon, when the stone glows golden. Look for the giant Nandi, carved from one stone, facing the shrine.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

16

Ramanathaswamy

Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu · Shiva as Ramanatha

Amazing facts

  • It is both one of the 12 Jyotirlingas and one of the Char Dham, a rare double honour.
  • Its pillared corridors are the longest of any Hindu temple in India, about 1.2 km (3,850 ft) in total, lined with some 1,212 carved pillars.
  • Inside are 22 holy wells (theerthams). Pilgrims are drenched with water from each before darshan. They are said to stand for the arrows in Rama's quiver, and people say each well's water tastes different.
The story

After defeating Ravana, Rama wished to worship Shiva. He sent Hanuman to Mount Kailash for a lingam; when Hanuman was late, Sita shaped one from sand. Both are worshipped: the sand lingam as Ramanatha, and Hanuman's as Vishwalingam, which by tradition receives worship first.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to April. Carry a change of clothes for the 22-well bath. Since September 2026 Tamil Nadu restricts smartphones in its major temples, so expect to leave yours at a counter for a small fee. Nearby: Dhanushkodi, the ghost town at the tip of the island, and the Pamban bridge across the sea.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

17

Sri Ranganathaswamy, Srirangam

Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu · Vishnu as Ranganatha, reclining

Amazing facts

  • It is often called the largest functioning Hindu temple in the world, spread over about 155 acres (63 hectares) including its temple town.
  • The temple has seven walls, one inside another, with people living, shopping and working between them like a town.
  • The Rajagopuram, completed only in 1987, is about 73 m tall with 13 tiers, one of the tallest temple towers in Asia.
  • It stands on an island between the Kaveri and Kollidam rivers and is the first of the 108 Divya Desams sacred to Vishnu.
The story

The image of Ranganatha was given by Rama to Vibhishana to take to Lanka. Vibhishana put it down at Srirangam to rest, and it would not move again. The Lord agreed to stay, but to face south, towards Lanka, to bless Vibhishana's land.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Vaikuntha Ekadasi (December–January) is the grand festival. Men may need a dhoti or traditional wear for the inner shrine. Climb the viewing terrace (small fee) for a view of the gopurams. Tamil Nadu restricts smartphones in its major temples since September 2026, so be ready to deposit yours.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

18

Sree Padmanabhaswamy

Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala · Vishnu reclining on the serpent Ananta

Amazing facts

  • The giant reclining image of Vishnu is so long that you see it through three doors: the face, the body and the feet.
  • When some underground vaults were opened in 2011 on court orders, they held gold, jewels and coins widely reported at the time to be worth about $22 billion. One vault (Vault B) is still unopened.
  • The city of Thiruvananthapuram takes its name from the deity: "the city of Lord Ananta."
The story

The image is made of 12,008 shaligram stones from the Gandaki river in Nepal, held together with a special herbal paste. The Travancore kings dedicated their kingdom to Padmanabha and ruled as his servants, the Padmanabha Dasas.

Planning a visit

Strict dress code: men wear a mundu or dhoti and no shirt; women wear a saree, set-mundu, half-saree or skirt and blouse. Dhotis can be hired at the entrance and worn over trousers or churidar. By temple tradition, only Hindus may enter. Phones and cameras are not allowed.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

19

Sabarimala

Pathanamthitta, Kerala · Ayyappa

Amazing facts

  • Pilgrims keep a 41-day vow (vratham) of simple living, wear black or blue, and climb barefoot through the forest.
  • Only those who have kept the vow and carry the sacred bundle (irumudi) on their heads climb the famous 18 holy steps.
  • The temple opens only in certain seasons, yet draws millions of pilgrims, making it one of the largest annual pilgrimages in the world.
  • All pilgrims call each other "Swami", whatever their background, as a sign that the divine is in everyone.
The story

Ayyappa was born of Shiva and Vishnu (as Mohini) to defeat the demoness Mahishi. Raised by the king of Pandalam, he later chose to sit in meditation on the hill of Sabarimala, where he is worshipped as a celibate yogi.

Planning a visit

Main season: mid-November to mid-January (Mandala–Makaravilakku), plus the first days of each Malayalam month; book a free virtual-queue slot on the official site, sabarimalaonline.org (compulsory in the main season; avoid look-alike sites that charge). Traditional rules restrict entry for women of certain ages; the wider legal question was heard by a nine-judge Supreme Court bench in 2026, and its decision was still awaited as of October 2026, so check the current rules before planning.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Pashupatinath

Kathmandu, Nepal · Shiva as Pashupati, Lord of all beings

Amazing facts

  • It is Nepal's most sacred Hindu temple, on the banks of the Bagmati river, and part of the Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • The main temple has a two-tiered golden pagoda roof and four silver-covered doors.
  • By a centuries-old custom, its chief priests, the Bhattas, come from South India.
  • On Mahashivratri, thousands of sadhus from Nepal and India gather here.
The story

One legend says Shiva once roamed this forest as a deer with Parvati, enjoying its peace. When the gods came to bring him back, they caught him by the horn, which broke and became a lingam. Another links it to the Kedarnath story: Shiva's head as the bull is said to have emerged here. Pashupati means the protector of all living beings.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to April. By tradition, only Hindus may enter the main courtyard; others can view it from the terraces across the river. Indian citizens enter free; other foreigners pay an entry fee. The open-air cremation ghats here are active, so be respectful and avoid photographing mourners.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Muktinath

Mustang, Nepal · Vishnu as Muktinath, Lord of liberation

Amazing facts

  • At about 3,700–3,800 m in the Himalayas (sources differ), it is one of the highest temples in the world.
  • Behind the temple are 108 stone spouts shaped like bull heads. Pilgrims run under the icy water of every one.
  • A small flame burns nearby from natural gas seeping out beside water, so earth, water and fire seem to meet in one place.
  • It is holy to both Hindus and Buddhists, who call it Chumig Gyatsa, "a hundred waters."
The story

Muktinath is one of the 108 Divya Desams of Vishnu. The nearby Kali Gandaki river is the source of shaligram stones, black fossils that are worshipped as forms of Vishnu, so pilgrims often bring one home.

Planning a visit

Best time: March to June and September to November. Reach Jomsom by small plane or road from Pokhara, then go by jeep. Altitude sickness is a real risk: go slowly and carry warm clothes. Indian citizens do not need a visa for Nepal, but the area is inside the Annapurna Conservation Area, so check whether you need its entry permit (ACAP) before you go.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Angkor Wat

Siem Reap, Cambodia · Built for Vishnu

Amazing facts

  • It is the largest religious monument in the world, covering about 163 hectares (400 acres).
  • King Suryavarman II built it between about 1122 and 1150 as a temple to Vishnu; it later became a Buddhist temple, which it remains today.
  • A carved wall about 49 m long shows the Samudra Manthan, the churning of the ocean of milk by gods and demons.
  • It is so important to Cambodia that it has appeared on the national flag since 1863.
The story

Its five towers stand for the five peaks of Mount Meru, the centre of the universe in Hindu cosmology, and the wide moat around it stands for the cosmic ocean. Unusually, it faces west, the direction linked with Vishnu and perhaps with the king's funeral.

Planning a visit

Best time: November to March. Watch the sunrise over the towers from the reflecting pool. Buy an Angkor Pass (1, 3 or 7 days); shoulders and knees must be covered to climb the upper level. Indians can get an e-visa online.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Prambanan

Central Java, Indonesia · Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva (the Trimurti)

Amazing facts

  • Built in the 9th century (inaugurated in 856 CE, according to an inscription), it is the largest Hindu temple site in Indonesia, with 240 temples originally.
  • The central Shiva temple is about 47 m tall, flanked by temples for Brahma and Vishnu.
  • Stone panels around it tell the Ramayana, and the Ramayana Ballet is staged several evenings a week, in an open-air theatre with the lit-up temple behind it during the dry season (May to October).
  • It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, carefully restored after a strong earthquake in 2006.
The story

A Javanese legend tells of Princess Roro Jonggrang, who agreed to marry a prince only if he built 1,000 temples in one night. As he neared the end, she tricked the roosters into crowing early. Furious, he turned her into stone as the thousandth statue: she is said to be the image of Durga in the Shiva temple.

Planning a visit

Best time: April to October (dry season). About 17 km from Yogyakarta; combine with the Buddhist Borobudur nearby. Go early morning or late afternoon to avoid heat.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Besakih

Bali, Indonesia · Shiva, Brahma and Vishnu in Balinese Hinduism

Amazing facts

  • Called the Mother Temple of Bali, it is a complex of 23 related temples, nearly 1,000 m up the slopes of Mount Agung, Bali's highest volcano.
  • When Mount Agung erupted in 1963, lava flows passed just metres from the temple, which Balinese see as a sign of divine protection.
  • Its tall, many-roofed shrines are called meru, named after the cosmic mountain; the number of roofs is always odd, up to eleven.
The story

Bali is the only island in Indonesia where most people are Hindu. Balinese Hinduism blends Indian Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions with local ancestor worship, and Besakih is its spiritual heart, where every Balinese family is linked to a shrine.

Planning a visit

Best time: April to October. A sarong and sash are required (usually lent at the entrance). Visitors may walk the courtyards but not enter the inner shrines. Ignore unofficial guides who insist on fees; use the official visitor centre.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Batu Caves

Selangor, Malaysia · Murugan (Kartikeya)

Amazing facts

  • A golden statue of Lord Murugan, about 43 m tall, stands at the entrance, the tallest statue of Murugan in the world, unveiled in 2006.
  • Pilgrims climb 272 steps, painted in rainbow colours in 2018, to reach the Temple Cave inside a limestone hill.
  • On Thaipusam, hundreds of thousands of people gather, by some counts over a million; many devotees carry decorated kavadis as offerings to Murugan.
The story

The caves are in limestone more than 400 million years old. A Murugan temple was set up inside in 1891, promoted by the Tamil community leader K. Thamboosamy Pillai, who is said to have felt the cave entrance looked like the vel, Murugan's spear. Tamil migrants brought the worship of Murugan to Malaysia, and Batu Caves became its centre.

Planning a visit

Open daily; free entry to the main Temple Cave. Thaipusam falls in January or February. Cover knees (sarongs can be hired). Watch your snacks: the monkeys on the steps are bold. About 13 km from Kuala Lumpur, reachable by commuter train.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Good to know

Visiting any temple

  • Take off your shoes before entering; most temples have a shoe stand.
  • Wear modest clothes that cover shoulders and knees; some temples require traditional dress.
  • Walk around the shrine clockwise (pradakshina), keeping the deity on your right.
  • Ask before taking photos; the inner shrine is usually off-limits for cameras.
  • Accept prasad with your right hand, and do not waste it.
  • Some temples allow only Hindus inside. Visitors are welcome to view from outside.
