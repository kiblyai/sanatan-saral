The Yoga of Knowledge

Every verse of chapter 2, सांख्ययोगः, with the Sanskrit, its pronunciation and a simple meaning in English and Hindi. Verses marked with a link are explained in depth on the main Gita page.

Verses 11–30 teach that the soul is eternal; 31–38 speak of Arjuna's duty as a warrior; 39–53 introduce karma yoga, acting without attachment to results; and 54–72 describe the sthitaprajna, the person of steady wisdom.

2.1 · Sanjaya

Gita 2.1

तं तथा कृपयाऽविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम् ।
विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः ॥

taṃ tathā kṛpayā'viṣṭamaśrupūrṇākulekṣaṇam | viṣīdantamidaṃ vākyamuvāca madhusūdanaḥ ||

Word by word
तं
to him (Arjuna)
तथा कृपया आविष्टम्
so overcome with pity
अश्रु-पूर्ण-आकुल-ईक्षणम्
eyes troubled and full of tears
विषीदन्तम्
grieving
इदं वाक्यम्
these words
उवाच
spoke
मधुसूदनः
Krishna (slayer of Madhu)
Translation

Sanjaya said: To Arjuna, overcome with pity, his eyes full of tears and his heart heavy, Krishna spoke these words.

2.2 · Krishna

Gita 2.2

श्री भगवानुवाच
कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।
अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥

kutastvā kaśmalamidaṃ viṣame samupasthitam | anāryajuṣṭamasvargyamakīrtikaramarjuna ||

Word by word
कुतः
from where?
त्वा
to you
कश्मलम् इदं
this weakness
विषमे
at this critical time
समुपस्थितम्
has come
अनार्य-जुष्टम्
unworthy of a noble person
अस्वर्ग्यम्
not leading to heaven
अकीर्ति-करम्
bringing disgrace
अर्जुन
O Arjuna
Translation

The Lord said: Where has this weakness come from at such a crucial moment, Arjuna? It does not suit a noble person, does not lead to heaven, and brings dishonour.

2.3 · Krishna

Gita 2.3

क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते ।
क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप ॥

klaibyaṃ mā sma gamaḥ pārtha naitattvayyupapadyate | kṣudraṃ hṛdayadaurbalyaṃ tyaktvottiṣṭha parantapa ||

Word by word
क्लैब्यं
cowardice, unmanliness
मा स्म गमः
do not give in to
पार्थ
O son of Pritha
न एतत् त्वयि उपपद्यते
this does not suit you
क्षुद्रं
petty
हृदय-दौर्बल्यं
weakness of heart
त्यक्त्वा
giving up
उत्तिष्ठ
stand up
परन्तप
O conqueror of enemies
Translation

Do not give in to cowardice, Arjuna; it is not worthy of you. Shake off this small weakness of heart and stand up, conqueror of enemies.

Gita 2.4

कथं भीष्ममहं संख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन ।
इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥

kathaṃ bhīṣmamahaṃ saṃkhye droṇaṃ ca madhusūdana | iṣubhiḥ pratiyotsyāmi pūjārhāvarisūdana ||

Word by word
कथं
how?
भीष्मम् अहं
I, against Bhishma
संख्ये
in battle
द्रोणं च
and Drona
मधुसूदन
O Krishna
इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि
shall I fight back with arrows
पूजा-अर्हौ
both worthy of worship
अरिसूदन
O destroyer of enemies
Translation

Arjuna said: How can I fight back with arrows against Bhishma and Drona, Madhusudana? They deserve my worship.

2.5 · Arjuna

Gita 2.5

गुरूनहत्वा हि महानुभावान्
श्रेयो भोक्तुं भैक्ष्यमपीह लोके ।
हत्वार्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव
भुञ्जीय भोगान् रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥

gurūnahatvā hi mahānubhāvān śreyo bhoktuṃ bhaikṣyamapīha loke | hatvārthakāmāṃstu gurūnihaiva bhuñjīya bhogān rudhirapradigdhān ||

Word by word
गुरून् अहत्वा हि
rather than killing my teachers
महा-अनुभावान्
noble, great-souled
श्रेयः
it is better
भोक्तुं भैक्ष्यम् अपि
to live even on alms
इह लोके
in this world
हत्वा अर्थ-कामान् तु गुरून्
but by killing teachers, even if greedy
इह एव
here itself
भुञ्जीय भोगान्
I would enjoy pleasures
रुधिर-प्रदिग्धान्
stained with blood
Translation

It would be better to live in this world by begging than to kill these great teachers. Even if they want gain, by killing them I would enjoy only pleasures stained with blood.

2.6 · Arjuna

Gita 2.6

न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो
यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।
यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषाम
स्तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥

na caitadvidmaḥ kataranno garīyo yadvā jayema yadi vā no jayeyuḥ | yāneva hatvā na jijīviṣāma ste'vasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ ||

Word by word
न च एतत् विद्मः
nor do we know
कतरत् नः गरीयः
which is better for us
यत् वा जयेम
whether we should win
यदि वा नः जयेयुः
or they should win over us
यान् एव हत्वा
those, by killing whom
न जिजीविषामः
we would not wish to live
ते अवस्थिताः प्रमुखे
they stand before us
धार्तराष्ट्राः
the sons of Dhritarashtra
Translation

We do not even know which is better: that we defeat them or that they defeat us. The sons of Dhritarashtra stand before us, and after killing them we would not even want to live.

2.7 · Arjuna

Gita 2.7

कार्पण्यदोषोपहतस्वभावः
पृच्छामि त्वां धर्मसंमूढचेताः ।
यच्छ्रेयः स्यान्निश्चितं ब्रूहि तन्मे
शिष्यस्तेऽहं शाधि मां त्वां प्रपन्नम् ॥

kārpaṇyadoṣopahatasvabhāvaḥ pṛcchāmi tvāṃ dharmasaṃmūḍhacetāḥ | yacchreyaḥ syānniścitaṃ brūhi tanme śiṣyaste'haṃ śādhi māṃ tvāṃ prapannam ||

Word by word
कार्पण्य-दोष-उपहत-स्वभावः
my nature overcome by the flaw of weakness
पृच्छामि त्वां
I ask you
धर्म-संमूढ-चेताः
confused about what is right
यत् श्रेयः स्यात्
what is truly good
निश्चितं ब्रूहि तत् मे
tell me that for certain
शिष्यः ते अहं
I am your student
शाधि मां
teach me
त्वां प्रपन्नम्
who has taken refuge in you
Translation

My nature is overcome by weakness and pity, and my mind is confused about what is right. Tell me clearly what is best for me. I am your student; I take refuge in you; teach me.

2.8 · Arjuna

Gita 2.8

न हि प्रपश्यामि ममापनुद्या
द्यच्छोकमुच्छोषणमिन्द्रियाणाम् ।
अवाप्य भूमावसपत्नमृद्धम्
राज्यं सुराणामपि चाधिपत्यम् ॥

na hi prapaśyāmi mamāpanudyā dyacchokamucchoṣaṇamindriyāṇām | avāpya bhūmāvasapatnamṛddham rājyaṃ surāṇāmapi cādhipatyam ||

Word by word
न हि प्रपश्यामि
I do not see
मम अपनुद्यात्
that could remove from me
यत् शोकम्
this grief
उच्छोषणम् इन्द्रियाणाम्
which dries up my senses
अवाप्य भूमौ
even gaining on earth
असपत्नम् ऋद्धम् राज्यं
a rich kingdom without rivals
सुराणाम् अपि च आधिपत्यम्
or even lordship over the gods
Translation

I see nothing that could take away this grief that is drying up my senses, not even an unrivalled, rich kingdom on earth or rule over the gods.

Gita 2.11

श्री भगवानुवाच
अशोच्यानन्वशोचस्त्वं प्रज्ञावादांश्च भाषसे ।

There was never a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor these kings; nor will any of us ever stop existing in the future.

यौवनं

Just as the soul in this body passes through childhood, youth and old age, so it passes on to another body. The wise are not confused by this.

Gita 2.14

मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः ।

Gita 2.20

न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचि
न्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।

📖 A story for this verse

From the Katha Upanishad

Young Nachiketa watched his father give away old, useless cows as a "gift" at a great sacrifice. "Father, to whom will you give me?" he kept asking. In anger his father said, "To Death!"

Nachiketa went to the house of Yama, the lord of death, and waited three days without food because Yama was away. To make up for it, Yama offered him three wishes. For the third, Nachiketa asked: "What happens to a person after death?"

Yama tried to change his mind with gold, kingdoms and long life. Nachiketa refused them all. Pleased, Yama taught him that the Self is never born and never dies, and is not killed when the body is killed. Krishna gives Arjuna the very same teaching, in almost the very same words, in this verse.

📖 A story for this verse

An everyday story

After Nana passed away, ten-year-old Tara would not stop crying. "Where did he go? Is he gone forever?"

Her mother opened Nana's cupboard and took out his old brown sweater, worn thin at the elbows. "Nana wore this every winter for twenty years. When it got too old, he bought a new one. Did Nana disappear when he took this sweater off?"

"No," Tara said slowly. "He was still Nana."

"Our tradition says the body is like this sweater," her mother said, "and the real person, the soul, is the one who wears it. In the Gita, Krishna says the soul puts down a worn-out body the way we put down old clothes, and takes a new one. The sweater is here. Nana is not in the sweater."

Tara held the sweater close. It still smelled of him. She missed him just as much, but she was not so afraid any more.

📖 A story for this verse

An everyday story

Meera had her first music recital. For weeks she kept asking, "What if I forget? What if nobody claps?" Her practice got worse, because her mind was always on the result.

Her grandmother said, "The claps are not in your hands. The practice is. Give each day's practice your full heart, and leave the rest."

Meera stopped counting days to the recital and simply played well every evening. On the day, she slipped once, smiled and carried on. She came home happy, not because of the applause, but because she had given her best.

That is the verse: do your work fully; the result is not yours to hold.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Valmiki Ramayana (Ayodhya Kanda)

One evening Rama was told, "Tomorrow you will be crowned king." The whole of Ayodhya was decorated with lamps and flowers.

The next morning everything changed. Queen Kaikeyi had held King Dasharatha to two old promises: her son Bharata would be king, and Rama would live in the forest for fourteen years.

Valmiki tells us that when Rama heard this, his face did not change at all. He was not proud at the thought of the crown, and not bitter at the thought of the forest. He bowed to his father and stepmother and calmly began to prepare to leave.

Being the same in success and failure: that is what this verse calls yoga.
