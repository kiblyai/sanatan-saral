In 30 seconds

  • A 700-verse conversation between Krishna and the warrior Arjuna, just before the Mahabharata war.
  • Arjuna doesn't want to fight his own family. Krishna teaches him how to act rightly without fear or selfishness.
  • Three paths run through it: selfless action (karma), loving devotion (bhakti) and self-knowledge (jnana).
  • Its most famous line (2.47): do your work, and let go of the results.
  • It ends with a promise of trust: "Take refuge in me; do not grieve" (18.66).

Before you begin

How to read the Gita

The Bhagavad Gita, "the Song of the Lord," is a conversation of 700 verses between Krishna and Arjuna, found in the Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata. It is set on a battlefield, but its real subject is how to live: how to act rightly when life is confusing, how to deal with fear and grief, and how to find God.

Traditional teachers divide its 18 chapters into three sets of six. The first six focus on action, the middle six on devotion, and the last six on knowledge. The map below lets you jump to any chapter. Each chapter has a short summary and its most important verses.

1

Arjuna's Despair

अर्जुनविषादयोगः

47 verses · Karma section

The two armies stand ready at Kurukshetra. The blind king Dhritarashtra asks his minister Sanjaya, who has been given divine sight, to describe what is happening. Arjuna asks Krishna to drive the chariot between the armies. Seeing his grandfather Bhishma, his teacher Drona, cousins and friends on the other side, Arjuna is overcome with grief. His bow slips from his hand and he sits down, refusing to fight.

The opening question

Gita 1.1

धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः ।
मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय ॥

dharmakṣetre kurukṣetre samavetā yuyutsavaḥ | māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāś caiva kim akurvata sañjaya ||

Word by word
धर्मक्षेत्रे
on the field of dharma
कुरुक्षेत्रे
at Kurukshetra
समवेताः युयुत्सवः
assembled, wishing to fight
मामकाः
mine (my sons)
पाण्डवाः च एव
and the Pandavas
किम् अकुर्वत
what did they do?
सञ्जय
O Sanjaya
Translation

Dhritarashtra said: O Sanjaya, gathered on the holy field of Kurukshetra, eager to fight, what did my sons and the sons of Pandu do?

In simple words

The Gita begins with the word dharma. Many teachers read the battlefield as the human heart, where good and selfish tendencies fight every day. Notice that the king says "my sons" and "Pandu's sons": the attachment that divides "mine" from "theirs" is the root of the whole war.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Mahabharata (Bhishma Parva)

Before the great war began, the sage Vyasa came to the blind king Dhritarashtra and offered him sight so he could watch the battle. The king refused. "I do not want to see my own family being killed," he said, "but I do want to hear everything."

So Vyasa gave the gift of divine sight to Sanjaya, the king's charioteer. Sitting in the palace, far from the field, Sanjaya could see every corner of Kurukshetra, even the thoughts in the warriors' minds.

The whole Gita is Sanjaya's report to the anxious king. It opens with this verse: a father's worried question, "What did my sons do?" The answer he gets is far bigger than a war report.

2

The Yoga of Knowledge

सांख्ययोगः

72 verses · Karma section

Arjuna becomes Krishna's student and asks for guidance. Krishna's teaching begins: the soul is eternal and cannot be killed, so do not grieve. Then he introduces karma yoga, acting without attachment to results, and ends by describing the sthitaprajna, the person whose mind stays steady in joy and sorrow. Many call this chapter the Gita in miniature.

The soul is never born and never dies

Gita 2.20

न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥

na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṃ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ | ajo nityaḥ śāśvato 'yaṃ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre ||

Word by word
न जायते
is not born
म्रियते वा
nor dies
कदाचित्
ever
न अयं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः
having been, it will not cease to be
अजः
unborn
नित्यः
eternal
शाश्वतः
everlasting
अयं पुराणः
this ancient one
न हन्यते
is not killed
हन्यमाने शरीरे
when the body is killed
Translation

The soul is never born, nor does it ever die. It did not come into being, and it will not cease to be. It is unborn, eternal, everlasting and ancient. It is not killed when the body is killed.

In simple words

Your body changes from baby to child to old person, but "you" remain. That unchanging you is the soul. Knowing this removes the deepest fear, the fear of death.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Katha Upanishad

Young Nachiketa watched his father give away old, useless cows as a "gift" at a great sacrifice. "Father, to whom will you give me?" he kept asking. In anger his father said, "To Death!"

Nachiketa went to the house of Yama, the lord of death, and waited three days without food because Yama was away. To make up for it, Yama offered him three wishes. For the third, Nachiketa asked: "What happens to a person after death?"

Yama tried to change his mind with gold, kingdoms and long life. Nachiketa refused them all. Pleased, Yama taught him that the Self is never born and never dies, and is not killed when the body is killed. Krishna gives Arjuna the very same teaching, in almost the very same words, in this verse.

Your right is to the work, not the fruit

Gita 2.47

कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥

karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana | mā karmaphalahetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi ||

Word by word
कर्मणि एव
in action alone
अधिकारः ते
is your right
मा फलेषु
never in its fruits
कदाचन
at any time
मा कर्म-फल-हेतुः भूः
do not let results be your motive
मा ते सङ्गः अस्तु
nor be attached
अकर्मणि
to not acting
Translation

You have a right to your work, but never to its fruits. Do not let the result be your motive, and do not become attached to inaction.

In simple words

Give your full effort, which you control. The result depends on many things you do not control, so don't let worry about it paralyse you, and don't use that as an excuse to stop working.

📖 A story for this verse

An everyday story

Meera had her first music recital. For weeks she kept asking, "What if I forget? What if nobody claps?" Her practice got worse, because her mind was always on the result.

Her grandmother said, "The claps are not in your hands. The practice is. Give each day's practice your full heart, and leave the rest."

Meera stopped counting days to the recital and simply played well every evening. On the day, she slipped once, smiled and carried on. She came home happy, not because of the applause, but because she had given her best.

That is the verse: do your work fully; the result is not yours to hold.

Yoga is evenness of mind

Gita 2.48

योगस्थः कुरु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा धनञ्जय ।
सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योः समो भूत्वा समत्वं योग उच्यते ॥

yogasthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā dhanañjaya | siddhyasiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā samatvaṃ yoga ucyate ||

Word by word
योग-स्थः
steady in yoga
कुरु कर्माणि
do your work
सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा
giving up attachment
धनञ्जय
O Arjuna
सिद्धि-असिद्ध्योः
in success and failure
समः भूत्वा
being the same
समत्वं
evenness of mind
योगः उच्यते
is called yoga
Translation

Steady in yoga, do your work, O Arjuna, giving up attachment. Be the same in success and failure. This evenness of mind is called yoga.

In simple words

Yoga is not only postures. It is a balanced mind: not over-excited by success, not crushed by failure. A calm mind also does better work.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Valmiki Ramayana (Ayodhya Kanda)

One evening Rama was told, "Tomorrow you will be crowned king." The whole of Ayodhya was decorated with lamps and flowers.

The next morning everything changed. Queen Kaikeyi had held King Dasharatha to two old promises: her son Bharata would be king, and Rama would live in the forest for fourteen years.

Valmiki tells us that when Rama heard this, his face did not change at all. He was not proud at the thought of the crown, and not bitter at the thought of the forest. He bowed to his father and stepmother and calmly began to prepare to leave.

Being the same in success and failure: that is what this verse calls yoga.

3

The Yoga of Action

कर्मयोगः

43 verses · Karma section

Arjuna asks: if knowledge is higher than action, why fight? Krishna explains that no one can live even a moment without acting. The answer is not to stop working but to work as an offering (yajna), for the good of the world (lokasangraha), without selfish desire. At the end Arjuna asks what drives people to do wrong; Krishna names desire and anger as the enemy.

People follow the example of leaders

Gita 3.21

यद्यदाचरति श्रेष्ठस्तत्तदेवेतरो जनः ।
स यत्प्रमाणं कुरुते लोकस्तदनुवर्तते ॥

yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ | sa yat pramāṇaṃ kurute lokas tad anuvartate ||

Word by word
यत् यत् आचरति
whatever (a person) does
श्रेष्ठः
a leader, a great person
तत् तत् एव
that very thing
इतरः जनः
other people (do)
सः यत् प्रमाणं कुरुते
whatever standard he sets
लोकः तत् अनुवर्तते
the world follows that
Translation

Whatever a great person does, others follow. Whatever standard they set, the world goes by it.

In simple words

Children copy parents, teams copy their leaders. If you are a parent, teacher or boss, your actions teach more than your words.

📖 A story for this verse

An everyday story

Every evening at dinner, Rohan's father said, "No phones at the table." And every evening Rohan saw his father's own phone buzzing beside his plate, and his father glancing at it. Rohan kept sneaking looks at his phone too.

One day his father noticed this and understood. The next evening he put his own phone in a basket by the door before sitting down. He did not say a word about it.

Within a week, Rohan's phone was in the basket too. Then his little sister's tablet joined them. Dinner became the best talk of the day.

Children, teams and whole societies copy what their leaders do, not what they say. That is this verse.

Better your own path than another's

Gita 3.35

श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात् ।
स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः ॥

śreyān svadharmo viguṇaḥ paradharmāt svanuṣṭhitāt | svadharme nidhanaṃ śreyaḥ paradharmo bhayāvahaḥ ||

Word by word
श्रेयान्
better
स्व-धर्मः
one's own duty
विगुणः
even if imperfect
पर-धर्मात्
than another's duty
सु-अनुष्ठितात्
even if well done
स्व-धर्मे निधनं श्रेयः
death in one's own duty is better
पर-धर्मः भय-आवहः
another's duty is full of danger
Translation

Better is one's own duty, even done imperfectly, than another's duty done well. Better to die doing one's own duty; another's duty is full of danger.

In simple words

Don't live a borrowed life by copying someone else's role. Do what is truly yours to do, according to your nature and responsibilities, even if you are not perfect at it.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional folk tale

A crow saw a swan gliding on a lake and thought, "How graceful! I will walk like a swan." For days the crow practised the swan's slow, swaying walk. It never got it right, and the other birds laughed.

When the crow gave up and tried to walk like a crow again, it found it had forgotten how. Now it could walk neither way and hopped about awkwardly.

The crow had its own gifts: it was clever, quick and never went hungry. By chasing someone else's path, it lost its own.

The verse says your own path, even walked imperfectly, is better than someone else's path walked well.

4

Knowledge and Renunciation of Action

ज्ञानकर्मसंन्यासयोगः

42 verses · Karma section

Krishna reveals that he taught this yoga long ago to the Sun god Vivasvan, and that he takes birth again and again to protect dharma. He explains that action done with knowledge does not bind: a wise person sees "inaction in action and action in inaction." Many kinds of yajna are described, and knowledge is called the greatest purifier.

Whenever dharma declines, I come

Gita 4.7–4.8

यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत ।
अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥
परित्राणाय साधूनां विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् ।
धर्मसंस्थापनार्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे ॥

yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata | abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṃ sṛjāmy aham || paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām | dharmasaṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge ||

ग्लानिः

भवति

अभ्युत्थानम्
rise
अधर्मस्य

Translation

Whenever righteousness declines and unrighteousness rises, O Arjuna, I manifest myself. To protect the good, to destroy the wicked, and to establish dharma, I appear in every age.

In simple words

This is the promise behind every avatar. However dark things get, goodness will be restored. It also asks us to stand on the side of dharma ourselves.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Bhagavata Purana

The demon king Hiranyakashipu won a boon that no man or animal could kill him, neither by day nor by night, neither inside nor outside, neither on earth nor in the sky, by no weapon. He declared himself god and punished anyone who prayed to Vishnu.

His own young son, Prahlada, would not stop. The king tried to have him thrown from cliffs, trampled by elephants and burned in fire, but Prahlada stayed calm and kept saying Vishnu's name.

At last the king struck a pillar and roared, "Is your Vishnu in this too?" The pillar burst open. Out came Narasimha, half man and half lion. At dusk, on the threshold, holding the king on his lap and using only his claws, he ended Hiranyakashipu's cruelty, without breaking a single word of the boon.

When good people are crushed and wrong grows strong, the Lord comes. That is the promise of this verse.

Nothing purifies like knowledge

Gita 4.38

न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते ।
तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति ॥

na hi jñānena sadṛśaṃ pavitram iha vidyate | tat svayaṃ yogasaṃsiddhaḥ kālenātmani vindati ||

Word by word
न हि
indeed there is not
ज्ञानेन सदृशं
anything equal to knowledge
पवित्रम्
purifying
इह विद्यते
here, in this world
तत् स्वयं
that, by oneself
योग-संसिद्धः
one perfected in yoga
कालेन

finds within oneself
Translation

Nothing in this world purifies like knowledge. One who is perfected in yoga finds it, in time, within the Self.

In simple words

Ignorance is the real dirt. Real understanding washes away fear, guilt and confusion, and it grows with steady practice over time.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional story of the sage Valmiki

Tradition tells that before he became a sage, Valmiki was a robber named Ratnakar who stopped travellers in the forest and took everything they had.

One day he stopped the sage Narada. Narada was not afraid. He asked one question: "You do this to feed your family. Will they also share the sin of it?" Ratnakar tied Narada to a tree and ran home to ask. Every person in his family said, "We share your earnings, not your sins."

That one moment of understanding changed him completely. He returned, fell at Narada's feet, and began a long life of meditation. The robber became the sage Valmiki, who later wrote the Ramayana.

Nothing purifies like true knowledge: one clear understanding can wash away a whole past.

5

Renunciation of Action

कर्मसंन्यासयोगः

29 verses · Karma section

Arjuna asks which is better: giving up work, or working selflessly? Krishna says both lead to the goal, but selfless work is easier and better for most people. True renunciation is inner: giving up selfish desire, not giving up duties. The wise see the same divine Self in all beings.

Like a lotus leaf on water

Gita 5.10

ब्रह्मण्याधाय कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा करोति यः ।
लिप्यते न स पापेन पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा ॥

brahmaṇy ādhāya karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā karoti yaḥ | lipyate na sa pāpena padmapatram ivāmbhasā ||

Word by word
ब्रह्मणि आधाय
offering to Brahman
कर्माणि
actions
सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा
giving up attachment
करोति यः
one who acts
लिप्यते न
is not stained
सः पापेन
he, by wrong
पद्म-पत्रम् इव
like a lotus leaf
अम्भसा
by water
Translation

One who acts by offering all actions to the Divine, giving up attachment, is not touched by sin, just as a lotus leaf is not wet by water.

In simple words

A lotus grows in muddy water but stays clean. You can live fully in the world, with family, work and money, without letting greed and ego stick to you.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Mahabharata (Shanti Parva)

King Janaka of Mithila ruled a rich kingdom, yet the sages called him a rajarshi, a king who was also a sage. He did all his royal duties with great care, but his heart was not tied to any of it.

The Mahabharata remembers a famous line of his, and storytellers set it in a scene: while Janaka was deep in a discussion of the Self, word came that his capital was on fire. Janaka said, "If Mithila burns, nothing of mine is burnt." He did not mean he did not care about his people; he meant that what is truly "his", the Self, cannot be burnt.

Like a lotus leaf that lives in water but is never wet, Janaka lived in the middle of wealth and power without being stained by them.

The wise see all as equal

Gita 5.18

विद्याविनयसम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि ।
शुनि चैव श्वपाके च पण्डिताः समदर्शिनः ॥

vidyāvinayasampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini | śuni caiva śvapāke ca paṇḍitāḥ samadarśinaḥ ||

Word by word
विद्या-विनय-सम्पन्ने
in one rich in learning and humility
ब्राह्मणे
in a Brahmin
गवि
in a cow
हस्तिनि
in an elephant
शुनि
in a dog
च एव श्व-पाके च
and in one who eats dogs

Translation

The truly wise see with an equal eye a learned and humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcaste.

In simple words

Real wisdom sees the same soul in every living being, whatever their caste, status or species. Looking down on anyone is a sign of ignorance, not knowledge.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional story of Adi Shankaracharya (Manisha Panchakam)

In Kashi, the great teacher Shankaracharya was walking back from his bath in the Ganga with his students. A man from the lowest caste, with four dogs, stood in the narrow lane. Shankara's students called out, "Move aside! Keep your distance!"

The man did not move. He asked calmly, "Whom do you want to move? This body made of food, from another body made of food? Or the pure awareness inside me, from the same awareness inside you?"

Shankara stopped. He had taught this truth for years, and here it was being taught back to him. He bowed to the man and composed five verses, saying that whoever truly sees the one Self in everyone is his teacher, whatever his birth.

That is the equal eye of the wise that this verse describes.

6

The Yoga of Meditation

आत्मसंयमयोगः (ध्यानयोगः)

47 verses · Karma section

Krishna gives practical instructions for meditation: a clean, quiet place, a steady seat, body straight, gaze inward, mind fixed on the Self. Balance in food, sleep and activity is essential. When Arjuna says the mind is as hard to control as the wind, Krishna agrees and gives the method: practice and detachment. He promises that no sincere effort is ever wasted.

Lift yourself by yourself

Gita 6.5

उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् ।

📖 A story for this verse

From the Bhagavata Purana

Five-year-old Dhruva tried to climb onto the lap of his father, King Uttanapada. His stepmother Suruchi pulled him away: "Only my son may sit there. If you want that place, pray to God to be born as my child!"

Dhruva ran crying to his mother Suniti. She did not blame anyone. She said, "Only the Lord can give you a place no one can take away."

The little boy walked into the forest. The sage Narada tested him, then taught him a mantra. Dhruva meditated with such determination that Vishnu himself appeared and gave him a place that would never move. Tradition says he shines today as the Pole Star.

No one lifted Dhruva up; he lifted himself. As the verse says, your own mind can be your best friend.

Balance in everything

📖 A story for this verse

An everyday story

Before her exams, Ananya decided to study through the night, skip meals and drink cup after cup of tea. By the third day she was reading the same page again and again. On exam morning her head ached and she forgot answers she knew.

Her friend Kavya studied every day, but she also ate on time, played badminton for half an hour in the evening, and slept by eleven. She walked into the exam fresh and calm.

For the next exams, Ananya tried Kavya's way. She studied fewer hours but remembered far more.

The verse says yoga is not for those who eat too much or too little, or sleep too much or too little. Balance in food, rest, work and play brings peace.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional story of the grammarian Varadaraja

Tradition tells of a boy named Varadaraja who could not remember his lessons. His classmates teased him, and at last even his teacher said gently that study might not be for him. Sad, he left the gurukul.

Thirsty on the way home, he stopped at a village well. He noticed deep grooves cut into the hard stone at its edge. They had been made by the soft rope of the water bucket, rubbing in the same place, day after day, for years.

"If a soft rope can cut stone by going over it again and again," he thought, "practice can surely get into my head." He went back and studied with new patience. He became a great scholar of Sanskrit grammar.

Krishna agrees that the mind is restless, but says it is tamed by practice and letting go.

The Imperishable Brahman

अक्षरब्रह्मयोगः

28 verses · Bhakti section

Arjuna asks about Brahman, the Self, karma and what happens at death. Krishna answers that whatever a person remembers at the moment of death, that they attain. Since we cannot choose our last thought at the last moment, we must remember God all through life. He also describes the cosmic cycles of creation and dissolution, the days and nights of Brahma.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional devotional legend

A poor scholar named Arjuna Mishra was writing a commentary on the Gita. When he reached this verse, "I carry what my devotees need," he hesitated. "Carry? Surely God only arranges it." With his pen he struck out the word "carry" and wrote "arrange".

That day there was no food in the house. He went out to beg and came back empty-handed. To his surprise, his wife was cooking. "A young dark-skinned boy brought rice, lentils and vegetables on his own head," she said. "He said your master sent them. But his lips were bleeding, and he said it was because you had cut him."

The scholar opened his book and saw the line he had struck through. He wept, and wrote "carry" back in.

The story is told to remind us: for those who rely on him fully, God carries the load himself.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Bhagavata Purana

Sudama and Krishna had studied together as boys in the ashram of the sage Sandipani. Years later Krishna was king of Dwarka, and Sudama was so poor that his family often went hungry.

His wife urged him to visit his old friend. Sudama had nothing to take as a gift except a few handfuls of flattened rice (poha), which she borrowed from neighbours and tied in a corner of his worn cloth. At the golden palace he was too ashamed to offer it.

Krishna ran out to meet him, washed his feet and sat him on his own seat. Then he noticed the little bundle, pulled it away playfully and ate a handful with great joy, as if it were the finest feast.

Sudama asked for nothing. But when he got home, his hut had become a fine house. A leaf, a flower, a fruit, a little water, or a handful of rice: what matters is the love.

Arjuna asks how to recognise God in the world. 📖 A story for this verse

A traditional folk tale

A sage was bathing in a river when he saw a scorpion struggling in the water. He scooped it up in his palm to carry it to the bank. The scorpion stung him, and fell back into the water.

He scooped it up again, and again it stung him. A man watching from the bank shouted, "Why do you keep saving a creature that keeps hurting you?"

The sage set the scorpion safely on the bank and said, "Stinging is its nature. Helping is mine. Why should I give up my nature because of its nature?"

He did not hurt the scorpion, and he did not let its sting disturb his peace. The verse describes such a person: one who disturbs no one, and is not disturbed by anyone.

13

The Field and the Knower of the Field

क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञविभागयोगः

34 verses · Jnana section

The third part of the Gita turns to knowledge. The body is called the field (kshetra), where actions are sown and their results harvested. The one who knows the field, the conscious Self, is the knower of the field (kshetrajna). Krishna lists the qualities that make up real knowledge, such as humility, non-violence, patience and purity, and explains prakriti (matter) and purusha (spirit).

I am the knower in every body

Gita 13.2

क्षेत्रज्ञं चापि मां विद्धि सर्वक्षेत्रेषु भारत ।
क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञयोर्ज्ञानं यत्तज्ज्ञानं मतं मम ॥

kṣetrajñaṃ cāpi māṃ viddhi sarvakṣetreṣu bhārata | kṣetrakṣetrajñayor jñānaṃ yat taj jñānaṃ mataṃ mama ||

Word by word
क्षेत्रज्ञं च अपि मां विद्धि
know me also as the knower of the field
सर्व-क्षेत्रेषु
in all fields (bodies)
भारत
O Arjuna
क्षेत्र-क्षेत्रज्ञयोः ज्ञानं
knowledge of the field and its knower
यत् तत् ज्ञानं मतं मम
that, in my view, is true knowledge
Translation

Know me also as the knower in all fields, O Arjuna. Knowledge of the field and its knower is what I consider true knowledge.

In simple words

You have a body, but you are the one who knows the body. Learning to tell "what I have" from "who I am" is the heart of spiritual knowledge. (Some editions number this verse 13.3.)

14

The Three Gunas

गुणत्रयविभागयोगः

27 verses · Jnana section

All of nature is made of three qualities, the gunas. Sattva is light, clarity and goodness; rajas is passion, activity and restlessness; tamas is darkness, laziness and confusion. They bind the soul in different ways. Krishna describes how to rise above all three and become gunatita, steady and free whatever happens.

The three qualities of nature

Gita 14.5

सत्त्वं रजस्तम इति गुणाः प्रकृतिसम्भवाः ।
निबध्नन्ति महाबाहो देहे देहिनमव्ययम् ॥

sattvaṃ rajas tama iti guṇāḥ prakṛtisambhavāḥ | nibadhnanti mahābāho dehe dehinam avyayam ||

Word by word
सत्त्वं
sattva (goodness)
रजः
rajas (passion)
तमः
tamas (inertia)
इति गुणाः
these qualities
प्रकृति-सम्भवाः
born of nature
निबध्नन्ति
bind

O mighty-armed

in the body
देहिनम् अव्ययम्
the imperishable dweller in the body
Translation

Sattva, rajas and tamas, the qualities born of nature, bind the imperishable soul to the body, O mighty-armed one.

In simple words

Even goodness can bind, through attachment to happiness and knowledge. First move from tamas to rajas (get active), then from rajas to sattva (get calm and clear), and finally go beyond all three.

15

The Supreme Person

पुरुषोत्तमयोगः

20 verses · Jnana section

Krishna describes the world as an eternal ashvattha (sacred fig) tree, upside down, with its roots above in God. Each soul is an eternal part of God. Krishna explains three persons: the perishable (all beings), the imperishable (the unchanging Self), and beyond both, the Supreme Person, Purushottama. Many families recite this short chapter before meals.

The upside-down tree

Gita 15.1

ऊर्ध्वमूलमधःशाखमश्वत्थं प्राहुरव्ययम् ।
छन्दांसि यस्य पर्णानि यस्तं वेद स वेदवित् ॥

ūrdhvamūlam adhaḥśākham aśvatthaṃ prāhur avyayam | chandāṃsi yasya parṇāni yas taṃ veda sa vedavit ||

Word by word
ऊर्ध्व-मूलम्
with roots above

knows the Vedas
Translation

They speak of an eternal ashvattha tree with roots above and branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns. One who knows it knows the Vedas.

In simple words

A normal tree draws life from the soil below. This world draws its life from God above. If we hold only the branches (things), we miss the root that gives them life.

16

Divine and Demonic Qualities

दैवासुरसम्पद्विभागयोगः

24 verses · Jnana section

Krishna lists the divine qualities that lead to freedom, such as fearlessness, purity, charity, self-control, truthfulness, non-violence and compassion, and the demonic qualities that lead to bondage: hypocrisy, arrogance, anger, harshness and ignorance. He names three gates to self-destruction and says to follow the guidance of scripture.

The first divine qualities

Fearlessness, purity of heart, steadiness in knowledge and yoga, charity, self-control, sacrifice, study of scripture, discipline and straightforwardness...

The very first divine quality is fearlessness. Notice that the list is about character, not rituals. This is a checklist anyone can use to grow.

17

The Three Kinds of Faith

श्रद्धात्रयविभागयोगः

28 verses · Jnana section

Faith also takes three forms, shaped by the gunas. Krishna shows how food, worship, discipline (tapas) and charity can each be sattvic, rajasic or tamasic. Sattvic food is fresh and nourishing; rajasic food is too spicy, sour or salty; tamasic food is stale. He ends with the sacred phrase Om Tat Sat.

You are what your faith is

sattvānurūpā sarvasya śraddhā bhavati bhārata | śraddhāmayo 'yaṃ puruṣo yo yacchraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ ||

Everyone's faith matches their nature, O Arjuna. A person is made of their faith: as their faith is, so are they.

What you deeply believe in and value shapes who you become. Choose carefully what you put your faith in.

Discipline of speech

Gita 17.15

अनुद्वेगकरं वाक्यं सत्यं प्रियहितं च यत् ।
स्वाध्यायाभ्यसनं चैव वाङ्मयं तप उच्यते ॥

anudvegakaraṃ vākyaṃ satyaṃ priyahitaṃ ca yat | svādhyāyābhyasanaṃ caiva vāṅmayaṃ tapa ucyate ||

Word by word
अनुद्वेग-करं
that does not upset
वाक्यं

Translation

Words that do not upset others, that are true, pleasant and helpful, together with regular recitation of scripture, are called the discipline of speech.

In simple words

Before you speak, ask four questions: Is it true? Is it kind? Is it helpful? Will it hurt someone needlessly? That is a spiritual practice you can do all day.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional folk tale

A boy with a quick temper often said hurtful things. His father gave him a bag of nails and said, "Each time you lose your temper, hammer one nail into the back fence."

On the first day he hammered in thirty nails. Slowly the number dropped, because holding back his words was easier than hammering. One day he hammered in none.

His father said, "Now pull out one nail for each day you keep your temper." After many weeks, all the nails were out. His father took him to the fence. "Well done. But look at the holes. The fence will never be the same. Harsh words leave holes like these in people, even after you say sorry."

The verse calls it a discipline of speech: words that are true, kind, helpful, and do not upset others.

18

Liberation through Renunciation

मोक्षसंन्यासयोगः

78 verses · Jnana section

The longest chapter brings together the whole teaching. Krishna explains true renunciation, the three kinds of knowledge, action, doer, intellect, determination and happiness, and the idea of doing one's own duty as worship. Then he gives his final, most secret teaching: surrender to me completely. Arjuna's confusion is gone; he picks up his bow and declares, "I will do as you say."

Take refuge in me alone

Gita 18.66

सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज ।
अहं त्वा सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥

sarvadharmān parityajya mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja | ahaṃ tvā sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ ||

Word by word
सर्वधर्मान्
all duties / all paths
परित्यज्य
having given up
माम् एकम्
me alone
शरणं व्रज
go for refuge
अहं त्वा
I ... you
सर्वपापेभ्यः
from all sins
मोक्षयिष्यामि
will set free
मा शुचः
do not grieve
Translation

Setting aside all other duties, take refuge in me alone. I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve.

In simple words

Called the charama shloka, the final verse of the teaching. After all the instructions, Krishna says: you don't have to carry it all alone. Trust me fully. Teachers explain it differently, but all agree it is a message of complete trust and comfort.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Mahabharata (Sabha Parva) and its well-loved retellings

After Yudhishthira lost everything in the game of dice, Draupadi was dragged into the royal hall. Duhshasana began to pull off her garment in front of everyone.

She looked to her husbands; they sat with heads bowed, bound by their losses. She looked to the elders, Bhishma and Drona; they were silent. She argued, she pleaded, and no one came forward.

The well-loved retellings say that she then stopped trying to hold on herself, raised her hands and called out with her whole heart, "Govinda! Krishna!" At that moment her garment began to grow without end. Duhshasana pulled and pulled until he fell exhausted, and she stood untouched.

"Set aside every other support and come to me alone. Do not grieve." This verse is Krishna's promise of that same rescue.
