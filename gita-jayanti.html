When

Gita Jayanti 2026

Gita Jayanti falls on Mokshada Ekadashi, the 11th day of the bright half of Margashirsha. In 2026 that is Sunday 20 December in India. Outside India, check your temple's calendar, because the tithi can fall a day apart.

  1. Sun 20 Dec 2026
    Gita Jayanti · Mokshada Ekadashi

    Tradition holds that on this day, on the field of Kurukshetra, Krishna taught Arjuna the 700 verses of the Gita.

Dates follow Drik Panchang for New Delhi and Raleigh, North Carolina. Your local temple may differ by a day, because tithis are calculated for local time.

Why we celebrate

The stories behind Gita Jayanti

A chariot between two armies

Arjuna asked Krishna to drive his chariot between the armies. Seeing his grandfather, teacher and cousins on the other side, he put down his bow. Krishna's answer to that moment of doubt is the Gita.

Read more →

Sanjaya, the first reporter

The blind king Dhritarashtra heard the whole Gita from his minister Sanjaya, whom Vyasa had given divine sight. Every reading of the Gita repeats that first telling.

Read more →

Why Mokshada?

Mokshada means "giver of liberation". A traditional story tells of King Vaikhanasa, whose fast on this Ekadashi freed his father from suffering. Many people fast or eat simply on this day.

Celebrated at Kurukshetra

Kurukshetra in Haryana holds a large Gita festival around this time, with recitation, lamps at Brahma Sarovar and talks. Temples everywhere hold group readings of all 18 chapters.

At home

Celebrate Gita Jayanti at home

Every family and region has its own way, so follow your elders where you can. This is a simple outline anyone can follow.

  1. Begin 18 days early

    Read one chapter a day from Thursday 3 December, and you finish chapter 18 on Gita Jayanti itself. Each chapter has a 1-minute summary on the Gita page, and every verse has its own page with word-by-word meanings.

  2. Set up a simple altar

    Place a copy of the Gita, a picture of Krishna and Arjuna, a lamp and a few flowers on a clean cloth.

  3. Say the Gita Dhyanam

    Before reading, say the opening prayer below. It honours the Gita as a mother.

  4. Read together

    Read chapter 12 (20 verses, on devotion) or chapter 15 (20 verses), or let each family member read one favourite verse aloud.

  5. Talk about one verse

    Pick one verse, such as 2.47, and ask: where could I use this at work or school this week?

Gita Dhyanam: the opening prayer

Gita Dhyanam 1 (traditional)

ॐ पार्थाय प्रतिबोधितां भगवता नारायणेन स्वयं
व्यासेन ग्रथितां पुराणमुनिना मध्ये महाभारतम् ।
अद्वैतामृतवर्षिणीं भगवतीमष्टादशाध्यायिनीम्
अम्ब त्वामनुसन्दधामि भगवद्गीते भवद्वेषिणीम् ॥

oṃ pārthāya pratibodhitāṃ bhagavatā nārāyaṇena svayaṃ
vyāsena grathitāṃ purāṇamuninā madhye mahābhāratam |
advaitāmṛtavarṣiṇīṃ bhagavatīm aṣṭādaśādhyāyinīm
amba tvām anusandadhāmi bhagavadgīte bhavadveṣiṇīm ||

Word by word
पार्थाय
to Partha (Arjuna)
प्रतिबोधिताम्
taught
नारायणेन स्वयम्
by Narayana himself
व्यासेन ग्रथिताम्
woven together by Vyasa
मध्ये महाभारतम्
in the middle of the Mahabharata
अद्वैत-अमृत-वर्षिणीम्
showering the nectar of oneness
अष्टादश-अध्यायिनीम्
of eighteen chapters
अम्ब
O mother
अनुसन्दधामि
I meditate on
भव-द्वेषिणीम्
who ends the cycle of rebirth
Translation

O Mother Bhagavad Gita, taught to Arjuna by Lord Narayana himself, set by the ancient sage Vyasa in the middle of the Mahabharata, showering the nectar of oneness, divine, in eighteen chapters, freeing us from the cycle of birth and death: I meditate on you.

In simple words

Many people say this before every Gita reading. Calling the Gita "mother" means it guides and protects us like a mother.
