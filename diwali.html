Five days of light

Diwali 2026, day by day

Diwali is a five-day festival in the month of Kartika. Dates below follow the Indian calendar for 2026. Because the festival follows the moon, in 2026 Choti Diwali and Lakshmi Puja fall on the same day, and there is a gap day on Monday 9 November.

  1. Fri 6 Nov
    Dhanteras

    Dhanvantari, the physician of the gods, rose from the churned ocean holding the pot of amrit. Families buy something new in metal for the home, and a lamp is lit at the door for Yama, praying for long life.

  2. Sun 8 Nov (morning)
    Naraka Chaturdashi · Choti Diwali

    Krishna, with Satyabhama, defeated the demon Narakasura and freed the 16,000 people he held captive. Many rise before dawn for an oil bath (abhyanga snana) and light a few lamps.

  3. Sun 8 Nov (evening)
    Diwali · Lakshmi Puja

    The new-moon night of Kartika. Ayodhya lit rows of lamps to welcome Rama, Sita and Lakshmana home after 14 years. Families worship Lakshmi, goddess of prosperity, with Ganesha, and fill the home with light.

  4. Tue 10 Nov
    Govardhan Puja · Annakut

    Krishna lifted Govardhan hill on his little finger to shelter the people of Vraja from Indra's storm. Temples offer a "mountain of food" (annakut). In Gujarat this is also the new year (more below).

  5. Wed 11 Nov
    Bhai Dooj

    Yamuna welcomed her brother Yama with a tilak and a meal, and he blessed every brother honoured this way. Sisters put a tilak on their brothers and pray for them; brothers give a gift.

Outside India? Your local temple may mark a day differently, because tithis are calculated for local time. Check its calendar for the exact puja time (muhurat).

Why we celebrate

The stories behind Diwali

Rama comes home

After 14 years in the forest and the defeat of Ravana, Rama returned to Ayodhya with Sita and Lakshmana. The people lit clay lamps along every street so the night was as bright as day. That welcome is why we light rows of lamps.

Read more →

Lakshmi rises from the ocean

When the gods and asuras churned the ocean of milk, many treasures came up. Lakshmi rose seated on a lotus and chose Vishnu. On Diwali night she is welcomed into clean, bright homes.

Read more →

Krishna and Narakasura

Narakasura troubled earth and heaven and held thousands captive. Krishna, with Satyabhama driving his chariot, defeated him and set the captives free. Light returning after fear is the heart of the festival.

Shared by many traditions

Jains celebrate the nirvana of Bhagwan Mahavira on this night. Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas, when Guru Hargobind was released from prison. In Bengal and Odisha the same night is Kali Puja.

At home

A simple Lakshmi Puja

Every family and region has its own way, so follow your elders where you can. This is a simple outline anyone can do in about 20 minutes.

  1. Clean and decorate

    Clean the home, put fresh flowers or a toran on the door, and draw a rangoli at the entrance to welcome Lakshmi.

  2. Set up the altar

    On a clean cloth, place a picture or murti of Lakshmi and Ganesha, a kalash of water, a lamp, flowers, rice, sweets and a few coins.

  3. Begin with Ganesha

    Light the lamp and pray to Ganesha first, who removes obstacles. Then say the lamp prayer below.

  4. Worship Lakshmi

    Offer flowers, rice, sweets and coins to Lakshmi while chanting her mantra 11 or 108 times, then sing the aarti your family knows.

  5. Fill the home with light

    Place lamps at the door, windows and every room. Share sweets with family and neighbours, and remember those who have less.

Govardhan · New Year

Govardhan, Annakut and the Gujarati New Year

Tuesday 10 November 2026 is Govardhan Puja. For Gujarati families it is also Besatu Varas, the first day of the new year, Vikram Samvat 2083. The greeting is "Saal Mubarak" or "Nutan Varshabhinandan".

Krishna lifts Govardhan

Young Krishna asked the people of Vraja to honour Govardhan hill, which fed their cows and gave them water, instead of holding the usual offering to Indra. Angry, Indra sent seven days of storm. Krishna lifted the whole hill on his little finger and everyone sheltered beneath it, until Indra's pride broke.

Read the story →

Annakut: a mountain of food

In memory of Govardhan, temples arrange dozens, sometimes hundreds, of dishes in a hill before the deity. Annakut darshan is a highlight in Pushtimarg (Haveli) and Swaminarayan temples. Check your local temple for darshan times; the food is shared as prasad.

A small Annakut at home

Cook a few dishes the family loves, sweet and savoury, and arrange them in a small heap around a picture of Krishna. Light a lamp, offer the food with a short prayer, then eat it together as prasad. Children can shape a little Govardhan hill from rice or play dough and stand toy cows around it.

Besatu Varas: the Gujarati New Year

Families wake early, wear new clothes, draw a fresh rangoli at the door and visit the temple. Younger members touch the feet of elders for blessings, and relatives visit one another with sweets and "Saal Mubarak". Many households buy a little salt first thing in the morning, called sabras ("all the good things"), for a year full of good flavours.

Labh Pancham, Saturday 14 November

The fifth day of the new year. Many Gujarati traders reopen their shops and new account books on this day, praying for labh (gain) and good fortune. Jains mark it as Gyan Panchami, honouring knowledge and scripture.

Lamp prayer

Traditional prayer (Shubham Karoti)

शुभं करोति कल्याणम् आरोग्यं धनसम्पदा ।
शत्रुबुद्धिविनाशाय दीपज्योतिर्नमोऽस्तु ते ॥
दीपो ज्योतिः परब्रह्म दीपो ज्योतिर्जनार्दनः ।
दीपो हरतु मे पापं दीपज्योतिर्नमोऽस्तु ते ॥

śubhaṃ karoti kalyāṇam ārogyaṃ dhanasampadā |
śatrubuddhivināśāya dīpajyotir namo'stu te ||
dīpo jyotiḥ parabrahma dīpo jyotir janārdanaḥ |
dīpo haratu me pāpaṃ dīpajyotir namo'stu te ||

Translation

Salutations to the light of the lamp, which brings good fortune, well-being, health and wealth, and destroys hostile thoughts. The lamp's light is the Supreme; the lamp's light is Vishnu. May the lamp remove my wrongs; salutations to the light of the lamp.

In simple words

Children in many homes say this every evening when the lamp is lit. On Diwali it is the perfect first prayer.

Lakshmi mantra

Traditional mantra

ॐ श्रीं महालक्ष्म्यै नमः ॥

oṃ śrīṃ mahālakṣmyai namaḥ ||

Word by word
ॐ
the sacred sound
श्रीं
seed sound of Lakshmi
महालक्ष्म्यै
to the great Lakshmi
नमः
salutations
Translation

Om, Shreem, salutations to the great Lakshmi.

In simple words

Chant it 11 or 108 times during the puja. Lakshmi stands for every kind of wealth: health, wisdom, kindness and enough to share, not money alone.

From darkness to light

Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.3.28

असतो मा सद्गमय ।
तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय ।
मृत्योर्मा अमृतं गमय ॥

asato mā sad gamaya |
tamaso mā jyotir gamaya |
mṛtyor mā amṛtaṃ gamaya ||

Word by word
असतः
from the unreal
मा
me
सत्
to the real
गमय
lead
तमसः
from darkness
ज्योतिः

मृत्योः
from death
अमृतम्

Translation

Lead me from the unreal to the real. Lead me from darkness to light. Lead me from death to immortality.

In simple words

This is what every Diwali lamp means: the outer light reminds us to grow the inner light of understanding.

Kids' corner

Diwali with children

Paint a diya

Get plain clay diyas and paint them with bright colours and dots.

Make a rangoli

Use coloured rice, chalk or flower petals to make a pattern at the door.

Tell the story

Read how Rama came home, then act it out with the family.

Share the light

Make a card or a plate of sweets for a neighbour or friend.

Safety first: keep lamps away from curtains and clothes, and let adults handle fireworks.
