In 30 seconds

  • Two great epics teach dharma through stories: the Ramayana and the Mahabharata.
  • The Ramayana (Valmiki, about 24,000 verses) follows Rama, the ideal son, husband and king, from exile to victory over Ravana.
  • The Mahabharata (Vyasa, about 100,000 verses) tells of a family war between cousins, and contains the Bhagavad Gita.
  • The Ramayana shows the ideal; the Mahabharata shows how hard right choices can be in real life.
  • Diwali, Dussehra and many other festivals come from these stories.

Itihasa

Two epics, one purpose

Itihasa means "so indeed it was." The two great epics teach dharma through stories everyone can follow, which is why most Indians learn their values from Rama and Krishna before they ever read a Veda. The Ramayana shows the ideal; the Mahabharata shows the real world, where good people face impossible choices.

The first poem

Ramayana: the seven kandas

Sage Valmiki's Ramayana, about 24,000 verses in seven books (kandas), is called the adi kavya, the first poem. It is the story of Rama, prince of Ayodhya, and it shows what an ideal son, brother, husband, friend and king looks like. Over the centuries it has been retold in almost every Indian language, including Kamban's Tamil Ramavataram, Krittivas's Bengali Ramayana and Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas, and across South-East Asia.

The first verse of poetry

Ramayana, Bala Kanda 2.15

मा निषाद प्रतिष्ठां त्वमगमः शाश्वतीः समाः ।
यत्क्रौञ्चमिथुनादेकमवधीः काममोहितम् ॥

mā niṣāda pratiṣṭhāṃ tvam agamaḥ śāśvatīḥ samāḥ | yat krauñcamithunād ekam avadhīḥ kāmamohitam ||

Word by word
मा निषाद
O hunter, no!
प्रतिष्ठां त्वम् अगमः
may you not find peace or standing
शाश्वतीः समाः
for endless years
यत्
because
क्रौञ्च-मिथुनात्
from a pair of krauncha birds
एकम् अवधीः
you killed one
काम-मोहितम्
lost in love
Translation

O hunter, may you find no peace for endless years, for you killed one of this pair of krauncha birds while they were lost in love.

In simple words

Valmiki saw a hunter shoot one of a pair of birds, and its mate's cry broke his heart. His grief (shoka) poured out in a new rhythm, and it became the first shloka. Brahma then told him to use this metre to tell Rama's story. Poetry, the tradition says, was born from compassion.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Valmiki Ramayana (Bala Kanda)

One morning the sage Valmiki went to bathe in the river Tamasa. In a tree nearby, a pair of krauncha birds sang to each other, happy together.

Suddenly a hunter's arrow struck the male bird. He fell to the ground, bleeding, and his mate circled above him, crying in grief.

Valmiki's heart filled with pain, and words burst out of him: "O hunter, may you find no peace for endless years, for you killed one of a loving pair." He was surprised: his grief had come out in perfect rhythm, a new kind of verse. He called it a shloka, because it was born from shoka, sorrow.

Then Brahma appeared and said, "Use this new verse to tell the story of Rama." That is how the Ramayana, the first great poem, began: with compassion for a bird.

Rama is dharma in human form

Ramayana, Aranya Kanda (Maricha's words)

रामो विग्रहवान् धर्मः ।

rāmo vigrahavān dharmaḥ |

Word by word
रामः
Rama
विग्रहवान्
in bodily form, embodied
धर्मः
dharma, righteousness
Translation

Rama is dharma in bodily form.

In simple words

Strikingly, this praise comes from Maricha, a demon and Rama's enemy, while warning Ravana not to fight Rama. Even opponents recognised his goodness.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Valmiki Ramayana (Aranya Kanda)

This famous line was not spoken by a devotee of Rama. It was spoken by the demon Maricha, to Ravana.

Years earlier, when Rama was only a boy guarding the sage Vishvamitra's yajna, Maricha had attacked it. Rama's arrow struck him and flung him far out over the ocean. Maricha never forgot it. He gave up his violent ways and lived as a hermit.

Now Ravana came to him with a plan: "Become a golden deer and lure Rama away, so I can carry off Sita." Maricha begged him to stop. "Rama is not cruel, nor foolish, nor greedy. Rama is dharma in human form. He is the king of all the world. Do not make him your enemy."

Ravana would not listen and threatened to kill Maricha. Maricha went, and died by Rama's arrow. Even Rama's enemies could see what Ravana could not.

The story, book by book

1. Bala Kanda
बालकाण्ड

Valmiki asks Narada who is the most virtuous man alive, and hears Rama's story. King Dasharatha of Ayodhya performs a yajna and his three queens give birth to Rama, Bharata, Lakshmana and Shatrughna. The sage Vishvamitra takes the young Rama and Lakshmana to protect his yajna from demons. In Mithila, Rama breaks Shiva's great bow and marries Sita.

LessonLearning from good teachers and protecting the weak are a young person's first duties.

2. Ayodhya Kanda
अयोध्याकाण्ड

On the eve of Rama's coronation, Queen Kaikeyi, influenced by her maid Manthara, claims two boons Dasharatha once promised her: Bharata as king, and Rama in the forest for 14 years. Rama accepts without anger. Sita and Lakshmana insist on going with him. Dasharatha dies of grief. Bharata refuses the throne, goes to bring Rama back, and when Rama will not break his father's word, rules as a regent with Rama's sandals on the throne.

LessonKeeping one's word, and the love of brothers who each try to give the throne to the other.

3. Aranya Kanda
अरण्यकाण्ड

Rama, Sita and Lakshmana live among the forest sages, protecting them, and settle at Panchavati. Ravana's sister Shurpanakha is rejected and humiliated. To take revenge, Ravana sends the demon Maricha disguised as a golden deer. Rama chases it, Lakshmana follows, and Ravana, disguised as a sage, carries Sita away to Lanka. The old vulture Jatayu dies fighting to save her.

LessonDesire for a "golden deer" can lead to great loss. Jatayu shows that courage is doing right even when you can't win.

4. Kishkindha Kanda
किष्किन्धाकाण्ड

Searching for Sita, Rama meets Hanuman and the exiled vanara prince Sugriva. Rama helps Sugriva defeat his brother Vali and win back his kingdom. Sugriva sends search parties in every direction. The southern party, led by Angada and Hanuman, learns from the vulture Sampati that Sita is in Lanka, across the ocean.

LessonTrue friends help each other in hard times. Hanuman's meeting with Rama begins the greatest story of devotion.

5. Sundara Kanda
सुन्दरकाण्ड

Hanuman remembers his strength and leaps across the ocean. He searches Lanka at night and finds Sita in the Ashoka grove, refusing Ravana's threats. He gives her Rama's ring and comforts her. Captured and brought before Ravana, he warns him to return Sita. When his tail is set on fire, he burns Lanka and leaps back with Sita's jewel as proof.

LessonHanuman did not know his own power until reminded. We often have more strength than we think. Many families read this kand for courage and hope.

6. Yuddha Kanda
युद्धकाण्ड

Ravana's brother Vibhishana advises returning Sita, is thrown out, and joins Rama. The vanaras build a bridge to Lanka. In the great war Lakshmana is struck down and Hanuman brings a whole mountain of healing herbs. Rama kills Kumbhakarna and finally Ravana. Vibhishana becomes king of Lanka. Sita proves her purity through fire, and they return to Ayodhya, where Rama is crowned.

LessonEgo and stubbornness destroyed Ravana, a learned and powerful king. Doing right sometimes means standing against your own family, as Vibhishana did.

7. Uttara Kanda
उत्तरकाण्ड

Rama rules as an ideal king; "Ram Rajya" comes to mean good government. Because of rumours among his subjects, Sita is sent to Valmiki's hermitage, where she gives birth to Lava and Kusha, who learn the Ramayana from Valmiki and sing it before Rama. Sita returns to the Earth, her mother. Scholars consider this book, like most of the first, a later addition.

LessonThe most debated part of the story. It raises hard questions about duty to the public versus duty to family, which people still discuss.

Rama's family

The great epic

Mahabharata: the eighteen parvas

Sage Vyasa's Mahabharata, with about 100,000 verses, is the longest poem in the world, roughly ten times the Iliad and Odyssey combined. It tells the story of the Bharata family, whose cousins, the Pandavas and Kauravas, fight a terrible war over the kingdom of Hastinapura. Around this main story are hundreds of other stories, long teachings on dharma and politics, and the Bhagavad Gita. It is called the fifth Veda.

Unlike the Ramayana's clear hero and villain, almost every character in the Mahabharata is a mix of good and bad. That is its great lesson: dharma is subtle (dharmasya tattvaṃ nihitaṃ guhāyām, "the truth of dharma is hidden in a cave"), and life's choices are rarely simple.

The greatest wonder

Mahabharata, Vana Parva (Yaksha Prashna)

अहन्यहनि भूतानि गच्छन्तीह यमालयम् ।
शेषाः स्थावरमिच्छन्ति किमाश्चर्यमतः परम् ॥

ahany ahani bhūtāni gacchantīha yamālayam | śeṣāḥ sthāvaram icchanti kim āścaryam ataḥ param ||

Word by word
अहनि अहनि
day after day
भूतानि
living beings
गच्छन्ति इह
go from here
यम-आलयम्
to the abode of Yama (death)
शेषाः
those who remain
स्थावरम् इच्छन्ति
wish to stay forever
किम् आश्चर्यम्
what wonder
अतः परम्
greater than this
Translation

Day after day, living beings go to the abode of Death. Yet those who remain want to live forever. What could be more wonderful than this?

In simple words

Four Pandavas drank from a lake without answering a yaksha's questions and fell dead. Yudhishthira answered every question. Asked "What is the greatest wonder?" he gave this answer. Pleased, the yaksha (Dharma himself, his father) restored his brothers. We all know we will die, yet we live as if we won't.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Mahabharata (Vana Parva, the Yaksha's questions)

During their exile, the Pandavas grew desperately thirsty in the forest. Nakula found a clear lake, but a voice said, "Answer my questions before you drink." He ignored it, drank, and fell dead. Sahadeva, Arjuna and Bhima came one by one, did the same and fell too.

Yudhishthira came last. He did not drink. He said, "Ask." The Yaksha asked many questions, and Yudhishthira answered each calmly. Then came: "What is the greatest wonder?"

Yudhishthira said, "Every day, creatures go to the house of Death. Yet those who remain behave as if they will live forever. What could be more wonderful?"

Pleased, the Yaksha offered to bring back one brother. Yudhishthira chose Nakula, Madri's son, "so that both my mothers have a living son". The Yaksha revealed himself as Dharma, Yudhishthira's own father, and brought all four back to life.

Connected:The Gita, also from the Mahabharata

Non-violence is the highest dharma

Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva

अहिंसा परमो धर्मः ।

ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ |

Word by word
अहिंसा
non-violence, not harming
परमः
the highest
धर्मः
duty, dharma
Translation

Non-violence is the highest dharma.

In simple words

This phrase appears several times in the Mahabharata and inspired Mahatma Gandhi. The epic, which describes a great war, also teaches that causing no harm is the highest value. Fighting was allowed only as a last resort, after every peaceful effort failed.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Mahabharata (Vana Parva, the story of King Shibi)

King Shibi was known for protecting every creature that came to him. To test him, the god Agni took the form of a dove and Indra the form of a hawk.

The frightened dove flew into the king's lap. The hawk landed beside it. "That dove is my food. You have no right to keep a hungry creature from its meal."

Shibi said, "I cannot hand over one who has come to me for shelter. Take any other food you want." The hawk said, "Then give me your own flesh, equal in weight to the dove."

A scale was brought. Shibi cut flesh from his own thigh and placed it on one side, with the dove on the other. The dove was heavier. He cut more, and more, and still the dove was heavier. At last Shibi stepped onto the scale himself.

The gods revealed themselves, healed him and blessed him. The great epic teaches that not harming, and protecting those who are weak, is the highest dharma.

What each character teaches

Lessons from the people of the Mahabharata

Yudhishthira

Truth and justice, but his weakness for gambling cost everything.

Bhima

Strength and loyalty; fiercely protective of his family.

Arjuna

Skill and focus; the student who asks the right questions.

Draupadi

Courage and dignity; she questions the whole court when wronged.

Karna

Generosity and loyalty, but loyalty to the wrong side.

Bhishma

A terrible vow kept at all costs, even when it meant standing by injustice.

Duryodhana

Jealousy and pride that would not give up even five villages.

Krishna

Friend, guide and God; works for dharma with wisdom and a smile.
