When

Maha Shivaratri 2027

Maha Shivaratri falls on the 14th night of the dark half of the lunar month (Phalguna in the north Indian calendar, Magha in the south Indian one: the same night). In 2027 it is Saturday 6 March in India and in the US.

Why we celebrate

The stories behind Maha Shivaratri

The pillar of light

Brahma and Vishnu once argued over who was greater. A pillar of fire appeared with no beginning and no end. Brahma flew up as a swan and Vishnu dug down as a boar, but neither found its end. Shiva appeared from the pillar: the first linga, a sign of the infinite.

The hunter in the bilva tree

A hunter, caught in the forest at night, climbed a bilva tree and stayed awake for fear of animals. All night he plucked leaves and dropped them, not knowing a Shiva linga stood below. Without meaning to, he had kept the Shivaratri vigil, and his heart changed.

Shiva and Parvati's wedding

Many traditions celebrate this night as the wedding of Shiva and Parvati, whose long devotion won Shiva's heart.

Markandeya conquers death

The boy Markandeya held a linga as Yama came for him, and Shiva protected him. The Mahamrityunjaya mantra is linked to his story.

At home

Celebrate Maha Shivaratri at home

Every family and region has its own way, so follow your elders where you can. This is a simple outline anyone can follow.

  1. Fast simply

    Many fast or eat only fruit and milk. Children, elders and anyone unwell should eat normally; devotion does not need hunger.

  2. A simple abhisheka

    At home, pour water, then a little milk, over a small linga or a picture, saying Om Namah Shivaya. Offer bilva (bael) leaves if you can find them.

  3. Chant

    Say Om Namah Shivaya 108 times with a mala, or the Mahamrityunjaya mantra 11 times.

  4. Stay awake (as you can)

    Temples hold worship in four watches through the night. At home, stay up for one watch with stories, bhajans or quiet meditation.

Mantra with meaning

Tap the speaker to hear it, and tap any Sanskrit word to see its meaning.

Om Namah Shivaya

Panchakshara mantra (Shukla Yajurveda 16.41)

ॐ नमः शिवाय ॥

oṃ namaḥ śivāya ||

Word by word
ॐ
the sacred sound
नमः
I bow
शिवाय
to Shiva, the auspicious one
Translation

Om, I bow to Shiva, the auspicious one.

In simple words

Called the five-syllable mantra (na-maḥ-śi-vā-ya). Its root is in the Shri Rudram of the Yajurveda ("namaḥ śivāya ca śivatarāya ca"). Easy for anyone to chant, any time.

Kids' corner

Maha Shivaratri with children

Count to 108

Make a mala from 108 beads or pasta tubes, and count your chants.

Draw the pillar of light

Draw the pillar going off both ends of the page, with a tiny swan and boar searching.

Night sky watch

Shivaratri is a moonless night: step outside with an adult and spot the brightest stars.

Story circle

Take turns telling the hunter story and the Markandeya story.
