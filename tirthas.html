Tirtha yatra

Pilgrimage circuits

A tirtha is a "crossing place": a spot where the boundary between the everyday world and the divine feels thin. Over centuries, Hindus have linked great tirthas into circuits that take a pilgrim across the whole of India, and sometimes beyond. This page covers five of the best known, with their stories and the places they include.

Where traditions disagree about a site or a list, we say so.

The four abodes

Char Dham

Four great temples at the four corners of India, linked by tradition to Adi Shankaracharya in the 8th century: Badrinath in the north, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east and Rameswaram in the south. Three are Vishnu temples and Rameswaram is a Shiva temple, a reminder that the circuit unites traditions. Visiting all four is one of the great pilgrimages of a lifetime.

Himalayan circuit

Chota Char Dham

Four shrines in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, visited from west to east: Yamunotri (the source of the Yamuna), Gangotri (the Ganga), Kedarnath (Shiva) and Badrinath (Vishnu). They open only from about April/May to November, and every pilgrim must register on the Uttarakhand government's site before setting out.

Yamunotri

Uttarkashi, Uttarakhand

Amazing facts

  • The shrine of the goddess Yamuna stands at about 3,290 m, near the source of the Yamuna river.
  • A hot spring beside the temple is hot enough to cook rice and potatoes; pilgrims cook rice in it, tied in cloth, and take it home as prasad.
  • The present temple was built in the 19th century by Maharaja Pratap Shah of Tehri Garhwal.
The story

Yamuna is the daughter of the Sun god and the sister of Yama, the god of death. It is believed that bathing in her waters protects one from an untimely death, and so the Himalayan yatra traditionally begins here, in the west, before moving east.

Planning a visit

Open from Akshaya Tritiya (April/May) to the day after Diwali; in winter the goddess is worshipped at Kharsali village. The last stretch is a trek of about 5–6 km from Janki Chatti (ponies and palkis available). Register for the Char Dham yatra on the Uttarakhand government's site.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Gangotri

Uttarkashi, Uttarakhand

Amazing facts

  • The temple of the goddess Ganga stands at about 3,100 m on the Bhagirathi river, as the Ganga is called here.
  • The actual source, the Gaumukh glacier, lies about 19 km further up the valley.
  • The temple was built in the 18th century by the Gorkha general Amar Singh Thapa.
The story

King Bhagiratha prayed for centuries to bring the heavenly Ganga down to earth to free the souls of his ancestors. Shiva caught the mighty river in his hair so that its fall would not shatter the earth. The rock where Bhagiratha is said to have prayed, Bhagirath Shila, is next to the temple.

Planning a visit

Open from about April/May to November; in winter the goddess is taken to Mukhba village. Reachable by road. Trekking to Gaumukh needs a permit from the forest department, and numbers are limited. Register for the Char Dham yatra on the Uttarakhand government's site.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Shiva as light

The 12 Jyotirlingas

The Shiva Purana tells how Brahma and Vishnu once argued over who was greater. Shiva appeared between them as an endless pillar of light. Vishnu flew down as a boar and Brahma up as a swan, and neither could find its end. The twelve Jyotirlingas, "lingams of light", are the places where Shiva is worshipped in that form.

Translation

The other eight

Mallikarjuna, Srisailam

Nandyal district, Andhra Pradesh

Amazing facts

  • It is both one of the 12 Jyotirlingas and one of the great Shakti Peethas: Shiva is worshipped as Mallikarjuna and Parvati as Bhramaramba.
  • The temple is enclosed by stone walls about 183 m by 152 m and 8.5 m high, carved with scenes from the epics.
  • The name comes from mallika, jasmine, the flower with which the lingam was worshipped.
  • 852 steps lead down to the Krishna river, called Patala Ganga here.
The story

When Kartikeya felt hurt that his brother Ganesha was married first, he went away to the Krauncha mountain. Shiva and Parvati followed to be near their son and settled on the Shrishaila hill, where they are worshipped as Mallikarjuna and Bhramaramba.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Mahashivratri is the biggest festival. The road passes through the Nallamala forest (a tiger reserve), so night travel may be restricted; check before you go. About 213 km from Hyderabad.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Mahakaleshwar, Ujjain

Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh

Amazing facts

  • It is the only one of the 12 Jyotirlingas whose lingam faces south (dakshinamukhi), the direction linked with death, which Shiva as Mahakala, the Lord of Time, conquers.
  • The pre-dawn Bhasma Aarti, held daily around 4 am, is famous across India. Passes are booked online on the temple's official site and sell out quickly.
  • The Nagchandreshwar shrine on the temple's top floor opens only one day a year, on Nag Panchami.
  • Ujjain is one of the four cities of the Kumbh Mela, held here as the Simhastha about every 12 years.
The story

A demon named Dushana, made invisible by a boon, attacked the city of the devoted King Chandrasena. Shiva burst forth from the earth as Mahakala, destroyed him, and at the devotees' request stayed on in Ujjain as its guardian deity.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. For the Bhasma Aarti, book on the official site (shrimahakaleshwar.com) about a month ahead and carry the same photo ID; men wear a dhoti and women a saree or salwar suit. The Mahakal Lok corridor around the temple is worth walking in the evening.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Omkareshwar

Khandwa district, Madhya Pradesh

Amazing facts

  • The temple stands on Mandhata island in the Narmada river, which is said to be shaped like the sacred syllable ॐ.
  • Pilgrims also visit Mamleshwar (Amaleshwar) on the opposite bank; together they are counted as the Jyotirlinga.
  • Tradition says the young Adi Shankaracharya met his guru Govinda Bhagavatpada in a cave here. A 108-foot statue of Shankaracharya, the Statue of Oneness, was unveiled on the island in 2023.
The story

The Vindhya mountain, wishing to grow greater, worshipped Shiva here with a lingam of sand and clay. Shiva appeared and blessed him. King Mandhata of Rama's Ikshvaku line also worshipped here, and the island is named after him.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Cross to the island by bridge or boat; many pilgrims walk the 7 km parikrama around it. About 80 km from Indore, the nearest airport; combine with Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Baidyanath Dham, Deoghar

Deoghar, Jharkhand

Amazing facts

  • The complex has 22 temples: the main shrine of Baba Baidyanath and 21 others.
  • During the month of Shravan, millions of kanwariyas walk barefoot to Deoghar carrying Ganga water from Sultanganj in Bihar, more than 100 km away, to pour on the lingam.
  • Some traditions instead identify the Jyotirlinga "Vaidyanath" with Parli Vaijnath in Maharashtra; the Jyotirlinga hymn itself names Parli.
The story

Ravana won from Shiva a lingam to take to Lanka, on the condition that it would stay wherever it was first set down. On the way he handed it to a shepherd for a moment; the shepherd put it on the ground, and it could not be lifted again. Shiva is worshipped here as Vaidyanath, the divine healer.

Planning a visit

Shravani Mela (July–August) is the biggest season and very crowded. Jasidih railway junction is about 7 km away and Deoghar airport about 9 km. October to March is calmer.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Bhimashankar

Pune district, Maharashtra

Amazing facts

  • The temple sits at about 930 m in the forests of the Sahyadri hills, where the Bhima river begins.
  • The surrounding forest is a wildlife sanctuary, home to the Indian giant squirrel, Maharashtra's state animal.
  • A large bell in front of the temple is a Portuguese church bell, given by Chimaji Appa, brother of Peshwa Bajirao I, after the Maratha victory at Vasai in 1739.
  • The hall and tower in their present form were built by the Maratha statesman Nana Phadnavis in the 18th century.
The story

Shiva took the fearsome form of Bhima Shankara to destroy the demon Tripurasura, who was troubling the three worlds. Tradition says the sweat that flowed from Shiva after the battle became the Bhima river. (A few other places, including one in Assam, also claim to be this Jyotirlinga.)

Planning a visit

About 110 km from Pune by road. The monsoon (July–September) is beautiful but misty and slippery; October to February is easiest. Mahashivratri draws large crowds.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Nageshwar

near Dwarka, Gujarat

Amazing facts

  • A seated Shiva statue about 25 m tall stands in the temple grounds and can be seen from the road.
  • It is about 17 km from Dwarka, so most pilgrims visit it along with Dwarkadhish and Bet Dwarka.
  • Its location is debated: Aundha Nagnath in Maharashtra and Jageshwar in Uttarakhand are also identified as the Nageshwar Jyotirlinga.
The story

The demon Daruka imprisoned the devotee Supriya and others in Darukavana, a forest-city by the sea. Supriya kept chanting Shiva's name; Shiva appeared, destroyed the demon and remained there as Nageshwar, the Lord of serpents.

Planning a visit

Best time: October to March. Easy to visit by road from Dwarka. Jamnagar airport is the nearest. Mahashivratri is the main festival.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Trimbakeshwar

Nashik district, Maharashtra

Amazing facts

  • Unusually, the lingam here has three small faces, for Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, set in a hollow in the floor of the sanctum.
  • The Godavari, the longest river of peninsular India, rises on the Brahmagiri hill above the temple; the Kushavarta pond nearby is treated as its source.
  • The black-stone temple was rebuilt in the 18th century by Peshwa Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb).
  • Nashik–Trimbakeshwar is one of the four sites of the Kumbh Mela.
The story

The sage Gautama, by mistake, caused the death of a cow and prayed to Shiva to bring the Ganga down to purify the place. Shiva released the Ganga here as the Godavari, also called Gautami, and stayed on as Tryambakeshwar, the three-eyed Lord.

Planning a visit

About 28 km from Nashik. Best from October to March; Mahashivratri and Shravan Mondays are very crowded. Special rituals such as Narayan Nagbali are performed only here. A crown with jewels is displayed on Mondays.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

Grishneshwar

Verul (Ellora), Maharashtra

Amazing facts

  • The temple stands about 1.5 km from the Ellora Caves, so it is easy to combine with the Kailasa temple.
  • Built of dark stone, its walls are covered with fine carvings of gods and stories.
  • It was restored by Maloji Bhosale, grandfather of Shivaji, in the 16th century, and rebuilt in its present form in the 18th century by the Holkar queens of Indore.
  • By local tradition, men remove their shirts before entering the inner shrine.
The story

The devoted Ghushma worshipped Shiva daily with clay lingams. Her jealous co-wife Sudeha killed Ghushma's son, but Ghushma kept praying calmly. Shiva brought the boy back to life, forgave Sudeha at Ghushma's request, and stayed here as Ghushmeshwar.

Planning a visit

About 30 km from Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad). Best from June to March. Note that the Ellora Caves next door are closed on Tuesdays, though the temple is open.

Timings, dress rules and entry rules change. Check the temple's official website before you go.

The Goddess

The 51 Shakti Peethas

When Sati gave up her life in the fire of her father Daksha's sacrifice, Shiva, mad with grief, carried her body across the universe. To end his sorrow, Vishnu's discus divided the body, and wherever a part or ornament fell became a Shakti Peetha, a seat of the Goddess. At each one the Goddess is worshipped with a Bhairava, a form of Shiva, as her guardian.

The number and the places differ between texts: 51, 52, 64 and 108 are all found. The table below follows one commonly cited list, based on the Pithanirnaya Tantra. Four are often called the Adi (first) Peethas: Kamakhya, Vimala at Puri, Kalighat and Tara Tarini in Odisha. Adi Shankaracharya's hymn names 18 Maha (great) Peethas, which include Kanchipuram, Srisailam, Kolhapur and Gaya. Several sites are now in Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Tibet and Sri Lanka.

Vishnu

The 108 Divya Desams

The Divya Desams ("divine places") are the 108 Vishnu temples praised in the hymns of the twelve Alvars, Tamil poet-saints who lived between about the 6th and 9th centuries. Their 4,000 verses, the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, are sung in these temples to this day. Srirangam is counted first. 105 are in India, one is in Nepal (Muktinath), and the last two are heavenly: the ocean of milk and Vaikuntha, which devotees believe are reached only after this life.
