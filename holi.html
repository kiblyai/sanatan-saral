When

Holi 2027

Holi comes on the full moon of Phalguna. The bonfire, Holika Dahan, is on the full-moon evening, and colours are played the next morning. In 2027: Holika Dahan on Sunday 21 March and Holi on Monday 22 March, in India and in the US.

Why we celebrate

The stories behind Holi

Prahlada and Holika

King Hiranyakashipu wanted everyone to worship him, but his son Prahlada prayed to Vishnu. The king's sister Holika, who had a cloak that fire could not burn, sat in a fire with Prahlada on her lap. The cloak flew to Prahlada; he walked out unhurt and Holika burned.

Krishna's colours

A popular story says young Krishna, worried that Radha was fair and he was dark, was told by his mother Yashoda to colour Radha's face any shade he liked. The playful colours of Vrindavan and Barsana come from this story.

Burning the old

In many places the bonfire also stands for burning away grudges and bad habits. On Holi morning people forgive, hug and start fresh.

Kama and Shiva

In parts of south India the day recalls Kamadeva, burned by Shiva's third eye and later restored by Shiva's grace after his wife Rati's prayers.

At home

Celebrate Holi at home

Every family and region has its own way, so follow your elders where you can. This is a simple outline anyone can follow.

  1. Light the bonfire safely

    Join a community bonfire, or light a small lamp at home instead. Offer a little grain and coconut and walk around it in prayer.

  2. Choose gentle colours

    Use dry, natural gulal or make colours from turmeric, beetroot and flowers. Oil your skin and hair first.

  3. Play with consent

    Ask before you colour anyone, keep colour away from eyes, and leave pets and anyone who says no alone.

  4. Share sweets, mend a friendship

    Make gujiya or any sweet, visit an elder, and call someone you have drifted from.

Prahlada's nine kinds of devotion

Bhagavata Purana 7.5.23

श्रवणं कीर्तनं विष्णोः स्मरणं पादसेवनम् ।
अर्चनं वन्दनं दास्यं सख्यमात्मनिवेदनम् ॥

śravaṇaṃ kīrtanaṃ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇaṃ pādasevanam |
arcanaṃ vandanaṃ dāsyaṃ sakhyam ātmanivedanam ||

Translation

Listening about Vishnu, singing of him, remembering him, serving his feet, worshipping him, bowing to him, serving him, being his friend, and offering him one's whole self.

In simple words

Little Prahlada said this to his father when asked what he had learned at school. It shows that devotion can be as simple as listening to a story or as deep as giving one's whole life.

Kids' corner

Holi with children

Make your own colours

Turmeric + flour for yellow, beetroot for pink, spinach for green. Dry them in the sun.

Act out Prahlada

One plays Prahlada, one Holika, one the king. Use a red scarf for the fire.

Colour-mixing lab

Mix two colours of water and guess the result before you pour.

Kindness rule

Agree on a family rule: ask first, no colour in eyes, no pets.
