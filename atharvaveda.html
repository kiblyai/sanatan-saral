The Veda of everyday life

What is the Atharvaveda?

Named after the ancient sages Atharvan and Angiras, the Atharvaveda is the Veda of ordinary life. Where the other three Vedas focus on the great public sacrifices, this one is full of prayers for a family's daily needs: health, healing, long life, a harmonious home, good harvests, safety and success. It also contains some of the most philosophical hymns in the Vedas.

It has 20 books (kandas), about 730 hymns and about 6,000 mantras in the main Shaunaka recension. A second recension, the Paippalada, survived in Kashmir and Odisha. Its priest is the brahman, who silently oversees the whole yajna and corrects mistakes, which is why it is also called the Brahmaveda. Its Upanishads, the Mundaka, Mandukya and Prashna, are central to Vedanta.

What it covers

Six themes of the Atharvaveda

ThemeWhat the hymns ask for
Health and healingCures for fever, cough, wounds and poison; praise of healing plants. Ayurveda draws on these.
Long lifePrayers to live a full hundred years in strength.
Home and familyHarmony between family members, a happy marriage (book 14), building a house.
Farming and animalsGood rain, healthy cattle, protection of crops.
Nation and kingA just ruler, unity of the people, protection of the land.
PhilosophyHymns to Skambha (the cosmic pillar, 10.7), Prana (life-breath, 11.4), Kala (Time, 19.53) and Mother Earth (12.1).

What holds up the earth (Bhumi Sukta)

Atharvaveda 12.1.1

सत्यं बृहदृतमुग्रं दीक्षा तपो ब्रह्म यज्ञः पृथिवीं धारयन्ति ।
सा नो भूतस्य भव्यस्य पत्न्युरुं लोकं पृथिवी नः कृणोतु ॥

satyaṃ bṛhad ṛtam ugraṃ dīkṣā tapo brahma yajñaḥ pṛthivīṃ dhārayanti | sā no bhūtasya bhavyasya patny uruṃ lokaṃ pṛthivī naḥ kṛṇotu ||

Word by word
सत्यं बृहत्
great truth
ऋतम् उग्रम्
mighty cosmic order
दीक्षा
commitment, consecration
तपः
discipline, austerity
ब्रह्म
sacred word, prayer
यज्ञः
sacrifice, selfless offering
पृथिवीं धारयन्ति
hold up the earth
उरुं लोकं कृणोतु
may she make wide space
Translation

Great truth, strong cosmic order, commitment, discipline, prayer and sacrifice: these hold up the earth. May she, the mistress of what was and what will be, make wide space for us.

In simple words

The earth is not held up by an elephant or a turtle, says this verse, but by values: truth, natural law, commitment and self-discipline. When people abandon these, the world becomes unstable.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Bhagavata Purana

The demon Hiranyaksha, brother of Hiranyakashipu, grew so proud of his strength that he seized the Earth herself and dragged her down to the bottom of the cosmic ocean.

As Brahma sat worrying about how creatures would live with no Earth to stand on, a tiny boar, no bigger than a thumb, sprang from his nostril. In moments it grew as huge as a mountain, roaring with joy. It was Vishnu, as Varaha.

Varaha dived deep into the dark waters and found the Earth. He fought Hiranyaksha, who blocked his way, and defeated him. Then he lifted the Earth gently on his tusks and carried her up to the surface, setting her back in her place so that life could grow on her again.

The Bhumi Sukta asks what holds the Earth up, and answers: truth, cosmic order, discipline and dedication. In the Puranas, that holding power takes the form of a boar who would not let the Earth sink.

A hymn for family harmony

Atharvaveda 3.30.1 and 3.30.3

सहृदयं सांमनस्यमविद्वेषं कृणोमि वः ।
अन्यो अन्यमभि हर्यत वत्सं जातमिवाघ्न्या ॥
मा भ्राता भ्रातरं द्विक्षन्मा स्वसारमुत स्वसा ।
सम्यञ्चः सव्रता भूत्वा वाचं वदत भद्रया ॥

sahṛdayaṃ sāṃmanasyam avidveṣaṃ kṛṇomi vaḥ | anyo anyam abhi haryata vatsaṃ jātam ivāghnyā || mā bhrātā bhrātaraṃ dvikṣan mā svasāram uta svasā | samyañcaḥ savratā bhūtvā vācaṃ vadata bhadrayā ||

Word by word
सहृदयं
one heart
सांमनस्यम्
one mind
अविद्वेषं
freedom from hatred
कृणोमि वः
I create for you
अन्यः अन्यम्
one another
अभि हर्यत
love, delight in
वत्सं जातम् इव
as for a new-born calf
अघ्न्या
the cow
मा भ्राता भ्रातरं द्विक्षत्
let brother not hate brother
मा स्वसारम् उत स्वसा
nor sister hate sister
सम्यञ्चः
united
सव्रताः भूत्वा
sharing the same aims
वाचं वदत भद्रया
speak kindly to one another
Translation

I make you one in heart and one in mind, free from hatred. Love one another as a cow loves her newborn calf. Let brother not hate brother, nor sister hate sister. United and of one purpose, speak to each other with kind words.

In simple words

Three thousand years old, and still the best family advice: love without conditions, no rivalry between siblings, shared goals and kind speech. The image of a cow licking her new calf captures selfless love.

📖 A story for this verse

A traditional folk tale

Two brothers farmed one field together and shared the harvest equally. The older brother had a wife and children; the younger lived alone.

One night the younger brother thought, "My brother has a whole family to feed. He needs more than I do." He quietly carried a sack of grain from his store to his brother's.

That same night the older brother thought, "I have my children to care for me when I am old. My brother has no one. He needs more than I do." He quietly carried a sack of grain to his brother's store.

Each morning both were puzzled to find their stores just as full. On the third night, carrying their sacks in the dark, they bumped into each other in the middle of the field. They understood at once, dropped the sacks and hugged.

"Love one another as a cow loves her newborn calf," prays the Atharvaveda. "Let brother not hate brother."

A prayer for fearlessness

Atharvaveda 19.15.6

अभयं मित्रादभयममित्रादभयं ज्ञातादभयं परोक्षात् ।
अभयं नक्तमभयं दिवा नः सर्वा आशा मम मित्रं भवन्तु ॥

abhayaṃ mitrād abhayam amitrād abhayaṃ jñātād abhayaṃ parokṣāt | abhayaṃ naktam abhayaṃ divā naḥ sarvā āśā mama mitraṃ bhavantu ||

Word by word
अभयं
fearlessness
मित्रात्
from friends
अमित्रात्
from foes
ज्ञातात्
from the known
परोक्षात्
from the unknown, the unseen
नक्तम्
by night
दिवा
by day
नः
for us
सर्वाः आशाः
all directions
मम मित्रं भवन्तु
may they be my friends
Translation

May we have no fear from friends, no fear from enemies, no fear from the known, no fear from the unknown. May we be fearless by night and by day. May all directions be my friends.

In simple words

A short prayer to say when you feel anxious. Fear comes from many sides, people we know and things we can't see. The prayer ends by asking that the whole world feel friendly.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Valmiki Ramayana (Kishkindha Kanda)

The search party for Sita reached the southern shore and stared at the ocean. Lanka was a hundred yojanas away across the water. One by one the monkey heroes said how far they could jump, and none could go that far. Fear and despair spread through them.

Hanuman sat apart, quiet. He did not remember his own strength.

Then old Jambavan, the wise bear king, went to him. "Why are you silent, Hanuman? You are the son of the Wind. As a child you leapt into the sky to catch the rising sun, thinking it was a fruit. Your strength equals that of Garuda. Rise!"

As Jambavan spoke, Hanuman began to grow. Remembering who he really was, he lost all fear. He climbed the Mahendra mountain and leapt across the ocean to Lanka.

The Atharvaveda prays for freedom from fear on every side. Often fearlessness begins when we remember our true strength.

Truth alone triumphs

Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.6

सत्यमेव जयते नानृतं सत्येन पन्था विततो देवयानः ।

satyam eva jayate nānṛtaṃ satyena panthā vitato devayānaḥ |

Word by word
सत्यम् एव
truth alone
जयते
wins
न अनृतं
not falsehood
सत्येन
by truth
पन्थाः
the path
विततः
is laid out
देवयानः
the path of the gods
Translation

Truth alone triumphs, not falsehood. By truth is laid the divine path.

In simple words

This line from an Atharvaveda Upanishad is India's national motto, written beneath the Lion Capital of Ashoka on every currency note and government seal.

📖 A story for this verse

From the Markandeya Purana and tradition

King Harishchandra was known for never breaking his word. The sage Vishvamitra decided to test him. When the king offered him anything he wished, the sage asked for his whole kingdom, along with an extra fee.

Harishchandra gave up his kingdom without argument. To pay the fee, he sold his wife and son as servants, and then sold himself to a man who kept the cremation ground in Kashi.

Years later, his son died of a snakebite. His wife, poor and alone, brought the boy's body to the cremation ground. Harishchandra recognised them. His heart broke, yet he told her that even she must pay the cremation fee, because that was his duty to his master.

At that moment the gods appeared, and Vishvamitra himself, revealing it had all been a test. The boy was restored to life and the kingdom given back.

"Satyameva jayate: truth alone triumphs." India chose these words from the Mundaka Upanishad as its national motto.

All this is Om

Mandukya Upanishad 1

ओमित्येतदक्षरमिदं सर्वं तस्योपव्याख्यानं
भूतं भवद्भविष्यदिति सर्वमोङ्कार एव ।

om ity etad akṣaram idaṃ sarvaṃ tasyopavyākhyānaṃ bhūtaṃ bhavad bhaviṣyad iti sarvam oṅkāra eva |

Word by word
ओम् इति एतत् अक्षरम्
this syllable Om
इदं सर्वं
is all this
तस्य उपव्याख्यानं
its explanation (follows)
भूतं
the past
भवत्
the present
भविष्यत्
the future
सर्वम् ओंकारः एव
all is Om alone
Translation

Om: this syllable is all this. Here is its explanation. The past, the present and the future: all of it is Om alone.

In simple words

The Mandukya, the shortest principal Upanishad, uses the three sounds of Om to explain three states of consciousness, and the silence after Om to point to the Self. See the diagram below.
