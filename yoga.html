What is yoga?

Joining, not just stretching

The word yoga comes from the Sanskrit root yuj, to join or to yoke. Today it often means stretching on a mat, but in our scriptures it means something much bigger: steadying the mind and joining the self with the Divine. The postures are one small part of it.

Three famous definitions, from three different scriptures:

Yoga is holding the senses steady

Katha Upanishad 2.3.11

तां योगमिति मन्यन्ते स्थिरामिन्द्रियधारणाम् ।
अप्रमत्तस्तदा भवति योगो हि प्रभवाप्ययौ ॥

tāṃ yogam iti manyante sthirām indriya-dhāraṇām | apramattas tadā bhavati yogo hi prabhavāpyayau ||

Translation

This firm holding of the senses is what they call yoga. Then one becomes watchful, for yoga can be gained and lost.

In simple words

This is one of the oldest uses of the word yoga, in an Upanishad: it is about mastering the senses, not about the body's flexibility.

Yoga is stilling the waves of the mind

Yoga Sutra 1.2

योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः ॥

yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ ||

Translation

Yoga is the stilling of the movements of the mind.

In simple words

Picture a lake. When the wind stops, the water becomes still and you can see the bottom clearly. Patanjali says the same about the mind.

Where it comes from

From the Upanishads to your living room

  1. The Vedas and Upanishads

    Several thousand years ago

    Seers meditate, control the breath and look within. The Katha and Shvetashvatara Upanishads describe yoga by name: sitting upright, calming the breath and steadying the senses.

  2. The Bhagavad Gita

    Part of the Mahabharata

    Krishna teaches yoga as a whole way of life: karma yoga (selfless work), bhakti yoga (devotion), jnana yoga (knowledge) and dhyana yoga (meditation, chapter 6).

  3. Patanjali's Yoga Sutras

    About 2,000 years ago (scholars' dates vary)

    196 short sutras that organise yoga into a clear system with eight limbs. Only three of the 196 sutras talk about posture at all.

  4. Hatha Yoga texts

    About 500 to 1,000 years ago

    Texts such as the Hatha Yoga Pradipika (15th century) describe many postures, breathing practices and cleansing methods, as a way to prepare the body for meditation.

  5. Yoga today

    The last 100 years

    Teachers in India and abroad made postures popular worldwide, and sequences like Surya Namaskar spread widely in the 20th century. Since 2015 the world marks International Day of Yoga every 21 June, after a United Nations resolution proposed by India.

Ashtanga yoga

The eight limbs of Patanjali

Notice the order. Patanjali starts with how we treat others and ourselves, and only then comes to posture and breath. A flexible body with an unkind heart is not yoga.

The eight limbs of yoga

Yoga Sutra 2.29

यमनियमासनप्राणायामप्रत्याहारधारणाध्यानसमाधयोऽष्टावङ्गानि ॥

yama-niyamāsana-prāṇāyāma-pratyāhāra-dhāraṇā-dhyāna-samādhayo 'ṣṭāv aṅgāni ||

Translation

Restraint, observance, posture, breath control, withdrawal of the senses, concentration, meditation and absorption are the eight limbs.

▶ Listen to recitationsShare on WhatsApp

1

Yama

How we treat others

Five restraints: non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness (satya), not stealing (asteya), self-control (brahmacharya) and not hoarding (aparigraha). Sutra 2.30.

2

Niyama

How we treat ourselves

Five observances: cleanliness (shaucha), contentment (santosha), discipline (tapas), self-study (svadhyaya) and surrender to God (ishvara pranidhana). Sutra 2.32.

3

Asana

A steady, easy posture

"Sthira sukham asanam" (2.46): the posture should be steady and comfortable. For Patanjali, asana mainly means a seat you can meditate in.

4

Pranayama

Working with the breath

Making the breath slow, smooth and steady. A calm breath brings a calm mind.

5

Pratyahara

Turning the senses inward

Like a tortoise drawing in its limbs, the senses stop chasing every sound and screen.

6

Dharana

Concentration

Holding the mind on one point: a mantra, a flame, the breath.

7

Dhyana

Meditation

When concentration flows without a break, like oil poured in a steady stream, it becomes meditation.

8

Samadhi

Complete absorption

The meditator, the act of meditating and the object become one. This is the goal of yoga.

From the Yoga Sutras

Four sutras to live by

Practice and letting go

Yoga Sutra 1.12

अभ्यासवैराग्याभ्यां तन्निरोधः ॥

abhyāsa-vairāgyābhyāṃ tan-nirodhaḥ ||

Translation

The mind is stilled through practice and non-attachment.

In simple words

Krishna says the same in Gita 6.35: the mind is restless, but practice and letting go tame it.

How practice becomes strong

Yoga Sutra 1.14

स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्कारासेवितो दृढभूमिः ॥

sa tu dīrgha-kāla-nairantarya-satkārāsevito dṛḍha-bhūmiḥ ||

Translation

Practice becomes firmly rooted when it is done for a long time, without breaks, and with care and respect.

In simple words

Ten minutes every day does more than two hours once a month.

Four attitudes for a clear mind

Yoga Sutra 1.33

मैत्रीकरुणामुदितोपेक्षाणां सुखदुःखपुण्यापुण्यविषयाणां भावनातश्चित्तप्रसादनम् ॥

maitrī-karuṇā-muditopekṣāṇāṃ sukha-duḥkha-puṇyāpuṇya-viṣayāṇāṃ bhāvanātaś citta-prasādanam ||

Translation

The mind becomes clear and calm by friendliness towards the happy, compassion towards those who suffer, joy towards the good, and calm distance from those who do wrong.

In simple words

A friend wins a prize: be glad, not jealous. Someone is hurting: help. Someone does good: cheer. Someone is rude: do not let it into your mind.

Steady and comfortable

Yoga Sutra 2.46

स्थिरसुखमासनम् ॥

sthira-sukham āsanam ||

Translation

Posture should be steady and comfortable.

In simple words

If a pose hurts or makes you strain, it is not yet your asana. Ease off.

Try it at home

A gentle 11-minute routine for the whole family

Nine simple steps, from a sitting Om to resting in shavasana. Press start and follow along; the timer moves you to the next step.

  1. Sit and chant Om

    1:00

    Sit cross-legged or on a chair, back straight. Chant Om three times, slowly.

  2. Neck and shoulder rolls

    1:00

    Slowly roll the shoulders back five times. Gently tilt the head to each side. No fast circles of the neck.

  3. Tadasana (mountain)

    1:00

    Stand tall, feet together. Raise the arms overhead and stretch up gently. Breathe slowly.

  4. Vrikshasana (tree)

    1:30

    Stand on one leg, the other foot on the calf or inner thigh (never on the knee). Hands together. Switch sides. Stay near a wall.

  5. Marjariasana (cat-cow)

    1:00

    On hands and knees: breathe in and let the belly drop, breathe out and round the back like a cat. Repeat slowly.

  6. Balasana (child's pose)

    1:00

    Sit back on your heels, fold forward and rest the forehead on the floor. Arms forward or beside you. Rest.

  7. Bhujangasana (cobra), gently

    1:00

    Lie on your front, hands under the shoulders. Lift the chest a little, elbows bent. Skip it if your back hurts or you are pregnant.

  8. Anulom vilom (alternate nostril breathing)

    2:00

    Sit up. Close the right nostril, breathe in through the left; close the left, breathe out through the right; then reverse. Slow and easy, with no holding of the breath.

  9. Shavasana (rest)

    2:00

    Lie on your back, arms by your sides, eyes closed. Let the whole body go loose. Just watch the breath.

Go gently. Breathe through the nose, never force a stretch, and stop if anything hurts. Children can do all of this with a parent. If you are pregnant, older, or have back, knee, eye (glaucoma) or blood-pressure problems, ask your doctor which poses suit you.

Honest answers

What modern research says

Based on the summary by the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH).

Fairly good evidence, modest benefit

Low-back pain: yoga helps a little, about as much as other exercise.

Promising, but studies are smaller

Neck pain, stress, anxiety symptoms, sleep, balance in older adults, and quality of life during cancer treatment.

Not a replacement for treatment

Yoga can help alongside medical care, but should not be used to put off seeing a doctor.

Safety: yoga is generally safe for healthy people when learned from a qualified teacher. Most injuries are minor sprains and strains. Beginners should avoid headstands, shoulder stands, the full lotus pose and forceful breathing. Hot yoga carries a risk of overheating.
