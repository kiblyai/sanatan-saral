Signs and symbols

Twelve sacred symbols explained

Symbols you see in temples, homes and festivals, with what they mean.

Om

The sacred sound said at the start of almost every prayer. The Mandukya Upanishad explains its three sounds, A-U-M, as waking, dreaming and deep sleep, and the silence after as pure awareness. It stands for Brahman, the whole of reality.

Read more →

Swastika

From su (good) and asti (it is): "may all be well." An ancient sign of good fortune, drawn in red on doorways, account books, wedding cards and at the start of any new venture, often with four dots. It is also sacred to Jains and Buddhists. In the 20th century the Nazi party took a tilted black version as its emblem, which is why it is misunderstood in the West; the Hindu swastika has a separate, thousands-of-years-old meaning of blessing and well-being.

Lotus (Padma)

The lotus grows in muddy water yet stays clean, so it stands for living in the world without being stained by it (Gita 5.10). Lakshmi and Sarasvati sit on lotuses, and Brahma is born from a lotus that grows from Vishnu's navel.

Conch (Shankha)

Blown at the start of worship and of battle, its sound is said to drive away negativity. Vishnu holds a conch, and in the Gita each warrior blows his own: Krishna's is Panchajanya (1.15).

Read more →

Sudarshana Chakra

Vishnu's spinning discus, the weapon that protects dharma. The wheel also stands for time and the turning order of the cosmos. A chakra (the Ashoka wheel) is at the centre of India's flag.

Trident (Trishula)

Shiva's three-pointed spear. Its three prongs are read as creation, preservation and dissolution; past, present and future; or the three gunas. Holding it means mastery over all three.

Lamp (Diya)

Light over darkness, knowledge over ignorance. A lamp is lit at dawn and dusk in many homes and at the start of every puja; on Diwali, rows of lamps welcome Rama home. "Tamaso ma jyotir gamaya": lead me from darkness to light.

Read more →

Kalash

A pot of water topped with mango leaves and a coconut, placed at the start of weddings, housewarmings and pujas. It stands for abundance, life and the presence of the divine; the water inside represents the sacred rivers.

Tilak

A mark on the forehead, where the "third eye" of wisdom is said to be. Followers of Shiva often wear three horizontal lines of ash (tripundra); followers of Vishnu wear a vertical U-shape (urdhva pundra). A simple red dot of kumkum is common for everyone.

Rudraksha

Seeds of the rudraksha tree, "the eye of Rudra," strung as prayer beads. A mala has 108 beads, used to count mantras in japa. It is especially dear to devotees of Shiva.

Tulsi (Holy basil)

A sacred plant grown in a raised pot (tulsi vrindavan) in many courtyards and watered every morning. It is dear to Vishnu and Krishna, and tulsi leaves are offered in worship. It is also valued in Ayurveda.

Bell (Ghanta)

Rung on entering a temple and during aarti. Its ringing is said to focus the mind, announce the worshipper and invite the divine presence.
